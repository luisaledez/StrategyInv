# Dexcom tripled earnings but lost its premium

*Research date: September 27, 2026. This is general equity research for an informed reader, not personalized investment advice. Price and GAAP financial data come first from the local SEC EDGAR and Yahoo price cache in this repository (`turnaround/cache/{edgar,fundamentals,valuation,prices}/DXCM.*`, summarized in [local_cache_price_and_ttm_data.md](<C:/Users/lledezma/MyProjects/StrategiesInv/research_notes/Dexcom DXCM deep dive/local_cache_price_and_ttm_data.md>)); non-GAAP figures come from Dexcom's 8-K press releases on EDGAR; consensus and analyst data come from third-party aggregators and are labeled as such.*

Dexcom's stock closed at **$86.62 on September 25, 2026, essentially unchanged (+0.7%) from $86.06 three years earlier**, even though trailing GAAP earnings per share rose **178% (from $0.91 to $2.53)** and trailing revenue rose **46% (from $3.40B to $4.97B)** over the same window ([local cache](<C:/Users/lledezma/MyProjects/StrategiesInv/research_notes/Dexcom DXCM deep dive/local_cache_price_and_ttm_data.md>)). The entire three-year round trip is multiple compression: the trailing P/E fell from roughly 95x to 34x, a drop of about 64%, which almost exactly cancels the earnings gain. That compression was not a single event but a sequence: a self-inflicted July 2024 guidance cut that erased 41% of the market value in one day, then a 2025 in which gross margin missed guidance twice, the FDA issued a warning letter, three Class I recalls landed, a short seller attacked the G7 sensor, the CEO went on unexplained medical leave, and management told the Street 2026 growth would be "slightly below" expectations. The stock bottomed at **$54.84 on November 10, 2025 (-61% from the April 2024 peak of $140.45)** and has since recovered 58% on proof rather than promises: three consecutive beat-and-raise quarters, non-GAAP gross margin back to 64.1%, record operating margin, record new-patient starts, and a $1B buyback. Dexcom has beaten consensus revenue in 15 of the last 16 quarters and consensus EPS in 14, yet four of those "beat" quarters produced next-day drops of 5% to 15%, because the market has been pricing the trajectory of gross margin and 2026-plus growth rather than the quarter itself. Looking 12 to 24 months out, a base case (about 50% probability) of 10-11% growth, mid-20s operating margin and a held 26-29x forward multiple points to roughly $90-105; a bear case (25%) of Abbott share pressure, ASP erosion and a stalled Medicare decision points to $55-70; a bull case (25%) of a favorable Medicare non-insulin type 2 coverage decision and margins tracking the 2030 targets points to $110-135. The probability-weighted value is around $95, a modest premium to today's price, which means the easy re-rating from the November 2025 trough has already happened and the next leg depends on catalysts that are external (CMS) or not yet visible (G8, international).

## A flat three years hides a 61% crash and a 58% recovery

The three-year price path is best read as four legs, each with a distinct cause, and the local price cache lets each leg be dated precisely. Between June and September 2023 the stock slid from **$128.51 to $93.30** as GLP-1 fears swept diabetes-device names; it then rallied 10.1% on October 27, 2023 after a Q3 2023 beat-and-raise with a $500M buyback and climbed to a **cycle high of $140.45 on April 9, 2024** ([local cache](<C:/Users/lledezma/MyProjects/StrategiesInv/research_notes/Dexcom DXCM deep dive/local_cache_price_and_ttm_data.md>); [Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/2173560/dexcom-dxcm-beats-on-q3-earnings-raises-sales-outlook)). At that peak the stock traded at about 91x trailing GAAP earnings on TTM EPS of $1.54, a multiple that required 20%-plus growth to persist. The second leg was the crash: on July 25, 2024 Dexcom missed Q2 revenue (~$1.00B vs ~$1.04B consensus) and cut FY2024 revenue guidance from $4.20-4.35B to $4.00-4.05B, and the stock fell **40.7% on July 26, its worst day since the 2005 IPO, erasing about $18B of market value** ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/07/26/dexcom-shares-plunge-more-than-40percent-after-q2-earnings.html)). Management attributed the shortfall to three U.S.-centric, execution-related causes: a sales-force realignment toward high-prescribing physicians that disrupted relationships, G7 rebate eligibility arriving "three times quicker" than for G6 and lowering revenue per patient, and share loss in the durable medical equipment (DME) channel; the company was roughly 70,000 new patient starts short of plan ([MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-q2-earnings-sales-forecast-shortfall/722540/); [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2024/07/30/dexcom-stock-plunged-eli-lilly-novo-nordisk/)). J.P. Morgan's Robbie Marcus said on the call he was "in shock at how big of a disruption and a downward guide it is," and Baird, J.P. Morgan and Morgan Stanley cut targets to $75-80 the same day ([MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-q2-earnings-sales-forecast-shortfall/722540/); [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/quote/DXCM/analyst-ratings)). The size of the move relative to a roughly 6% guidance cut reflected the starting multiple and the loss of credibility: guidance had been raised only three months earlier.

The third leg, from August 2024 through November 2025, was a grind lower driven by margin and quality rather than revenue. Revenue never fell year-over-year in any quarter; the worst print was +2% in Q3 2024. But Q4 2024 non-GAAP gross margin collapsed to 59.4% from 64.2% on inventory damaged in transit and low-yield build configurations ([Dexcom 8-K, Feb 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355725000029/dxcm12312024-exhibit991.htm)); an FDA warning letter dated March 4, 2025 cited an uncleared change to the sensor resistance-layer material and knocked the stock 9.1% on March 10 ([Dexcom 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355725000047/dxcm-20250304.htm); [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/dexcom-receives-fda-warning-letter-over-cgm-manufacturing-issues)); the FY2025 gross-margin guide was cut from 64-65% to 62% in May and to 61% in October as expedited air freight and scrap from extra component scrutiny cost "a few hundred basis points" ([Q1 2025 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355725000111/dxcm03312025-exhibit991.htm); [Investing.com Q3 2025 call](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-dexcom-beats-q3-2025-estimates-stock-steady-93CH-4322136)). Then came the September 2025 cluster: CEO Kevin Sayer took an unplanned medical leave on September 14, and four days later Hunterbrook Media published "Dexcom's Fatal Flaws," alleging the G7 coating change was inferior on every accuracy metric; the stock fell **11.8% over two days to $67.45** ([Dexcom 8-K, Sept 14, 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355725000257/dxcm-20250914.htm); [Hunterbrook](https://hntrbrk.com/dexcom/); [Labaton](https://www.labaton.com/news-insights/labaton-keller-sucharow-llp-announces-expanded-securities-class-action-lawsuit-filed-against-dexcom-inc-and-certain-executives)). The Q3 2025 print on October 30 was a strong beat (+22% revenue) but paired a second gross-margin guide cut with interim CEO Jake Leach's comment that the top of the preliminary 2026 range was "probably slightly below where the Street is today," and an admission that the short report had dented new starts; the stock fell **14.6% to $58.59, its lowest since March 2020 and the worst in the S&P 500 that day** ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-dexcom-beats-q3-2025-estimates-stock-steady-93CH-4322136); [Barron's via iTiger](https://www.itiger.com/news/2579498121)). The trough of $54.84 on November 10 coincided with maximum uncertainty on three axes at once (leadership, quality, reimbursement), the last because CMS finalized a rule on November 28 moving CGMs into Medicare competitive bidding from 2028 ([MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/cms-final-rule-diabetes-competitive-bidding/806719/)).

The fourth leg is the 2026 recovery. Morgan Stanley upgraded to Overweight on December 2, 2025, arguing investors underestimated the G7 margin opportunity ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/morgan-stanley-upgrades-dexcom-dxcm-134210351.html)); G7 15 Day launched in the U.S. on December 1; the Q4 2025 print (February 12, 2026) showed non-GAAP gross margin back to 63.5% and FCF above $1B, and the stock rose 7.6% ([Q4 2025 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355726000025/dxcm12312025-exhibit991.htm)). The stock nonetheless drifted back to **$59.55 at the end of April 2026** on a Barclays Underweight (January 12) citing Abbott's share gains in insulin-intensive segments, a 2026 guide whose midpoint sat below LSEG consensus, and an in-line Q1 print ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/analyst-ratings/barclays-downgrades-dexcom-stock-rating-to-underweight-on-market-share-concerns-93CH-4442644); [Medical Device Network](https://www.medicaldevice-network.com/news/dexcom-reports-2025-revenue-of-4-bn-yet-2026-outlook-lags-analyst-expectations/)). What changed the narrative was the May 14, 2026 Investor Day (2030 targets, $1B buyback, a settlement with Elliott, +6.6% the next day) and above all the **Q2 2026 print on July 30: revenue +13%, non-GAAP gross margin 64.1% versus 60.1%, GAAP operating margin 24.3%, all four guidance metrics raised, record new starts, and a 12.0% gain the next day** ([Dexcom 8-K, Q2 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001093557/000109355726000142/dxcm06302026-exhibit991.htm); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/dexcom-dxcm-posts-upbeat-2026-014357618.html)). The stock reached **$92.34 on August 21, 2026** before easing to $86.62 ([ad-hoc-news](https://www.ad-hoc-news.de/boerse/news/corporate-news/dexcom-stock-trades-close-to-52-week-high-as-q2-2026-growth-and-analyst/69992583)).

Over the full three years, **EPS rose 178% while the multiple fell 64%, leaving the price up 0.7%**; from the April 2024 peak, EPS rose 64% while the multiple fell 63%, leaving the price down 38%; from the peak to the trough, EPS still rose 17% while the multiple fell 67%. Since the trough, EPS is up 41% and the multiple has only recovered about 12% (30x to 34x), so most of the 58% rebound is earnings, not re-rating. The local cache puts the current trailing P/E at the 9th percentile of Dexcom's own monthly history since 2013 (median 90x, five-year median 83x), and the month-end P/E series shows the staircase down: 75.7x (June 2024), 44.7x (September 2024), 65.6x (June 2025), 47.4x (September 2025), 36.9x (December 2025), 30.0x (March 2026), 28.9x (June 2026), 32.8x (September 2026) ([local cache](<C:/Users/lledezma/MyProjects/StrategiesInv/research_notes/Dexcom DXCM deep dive/local_cache_price_and_ttm_data.md>)). Third-party sources agree on the shape: FullRatio's 3-, 5- and 10-year average P/Es are 53x, 71x and 58x and GuruFocus's five-year median is 89.6x against 35.2x today ([FullRatio](https://fullratio.com/stocks/nasdaq-dxcm/pe-ratio); [GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9088419/dxcm-maintained-by-truist-securities-price-target-raised-to-102)). Two caveats make the historical averages a poor re-rating anchor: Dexcom was only marginally profitable on a GAAP basis through 2020-2023, inflating its trailing P/E, and the growth rate that justified 70-90x (24% in 2023) has downshifted to 11-16%. On a forward basis, the multiple went from roughly 26x at the October-November 2025 low to about 28-31x now (the local cache's forward P/E is 27.7x on forward EPS of $3.12; StockAnalysis shows 30.7x), so the 2026 rally has delivered roughly 20-25% multiple expansion on top of estimate upgrades ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2025/11/12/dexcom-stock-just-fell-to-5-year-low-is-it-a-buy); [StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/dxcm/statistics/)). The stock also remains **22.6% below the pre-cut close of $111.94 (July 24, 2024) despite TTM revenue being 26% higher** and 47% below its all-time high of $162.82 (November 17, 2021) ([local cache](<C:/Users/lledezma/MyProjects/StrategiesInv/research_notes/Dexcom DXCM deep dive/local_cache_price_and_ttm_data.md>)).

Why did the multiple compress so far? Three forces compounded. Growth downshifted from the 20%-plus that the 2024 multiple assumed to 11% (2024), 16% (2025) and a guided 11-13% (2026). Execution credibility was damaged repeatedly: the July 2024 cut came three months after a raise, the FY2025 gross-margin guide was cut twice and still missed (60.8% actual vs an initial 64-65%), and the first framing of 2026 was explicitly "below the Street." And two structural overhangs appeared that had no revenue impact yet but capped the multiple: Abbott's share momentum, including its exclusive Medtronic pump tie-up, and the CMS competitive-bidding rule for 2028. The one-year and two-year returns (+26.8% and +29.8%) show that the recovery has been real but partial; the notes contain no three-year benchmark return, though Zacks reported DXCM +34.8% year-to-date through August 12, 2026 versus +13.1% for the S&P 500 and -6.9% for the healthcare industry ([Yahoo/Zacks](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/dxcm-stock-nearly-35-ytd-183400790.html)).

## Sixteen quarters: fifteen revenue beats, four of them punished

The quarter-by-quarter record from Q3 2022 through Q2 2026 shows revenue climbing from $770M to $1,308M, but along a path that accelerated to 24-27% through 2023, collapsed to 2% in Q3 2024, re-accelerated to 22% in Q3 2025 (against an easy comparison) and settled at 12-13% organic in 2026. Non-GAAP gross margin fell from 64-67% in 2022-2023 to a trough of **57.5% in Q1 2025** and has recovered to **64.1% in Q2 2026**; non-GAAP operating margin has expanded to a record 25-26% in two of the last three quarters. ### Guidance evolution: one cut, two margin misses, then a year of raises

FY2023 was a textbook beat-and-raise year: initial guidance of $3.35-3.49B was raised three times to $3.575-3.60B and actual revenue was $3.622B, with non-GAAP gross margin finishing at 64.0% against an initial 62-63% ([Q4 2022 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355723000022/dxcmq4202299-1_12312022.htm); [Q4 2023 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355724000020/dxcmq4202399-1_12312023.htm)). FY2024 started at $4.15-4.35B, was nudged up in April, cut to $4.00-4.05B in July, and finished at $4.033B with every margin metric missing (non-GAAP GM 61.8% vs ~63%, OM 18.8% vs ~20%, adjusted EBITDA 27.4% vs ~29%) ([Q2 2024 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355724000143/dxcmq2202499-1_6302024.htm); [Q4 2024 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355725000029/dxcm12312024-exhibit991.htm)). FY2025 was the mirror image: revenue was raised twice (to $4.63-4.65B; actual $4.662B) while the gross-margin guide was cut from 64-65% to 62% in May (a bridge of 75 bp Q1 shortfall, 100 bp expedited freight, 50 bp tariffs, 25 bp FX) and to 61% in October, and the year still came in at 60.8% ([Q1 2025 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355725000111/dxcm03312025-exhibit991.htm); [Q3 2025 release](https://investors.dexcom.com/news/news-details/2025/Dexcom-Reports-Third-Quarter-2025-Financial-Results-and-Updates-Full-Year-2025-Guidance/default.aspx)). FY2026 is the first year since 2023 in which Dexcom is tracking ahead of its own margin guide: the initial $5.16-5.25B / 63-64% GM / 22-23% OM was raised in April (OM 23-23.5%) and again in July to **$5.18-5.25B revenue, ~64% GM, 23.5-24% OM and 31.5-32% adjusted EBITDA margin** ([Q2 2026 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001093557/000109355726000142/dxcm06302026-exhibit991.htm)). The pattern is telling: management has used margin as the lever for raises while holding revenue near the original range, which reads as a deliberate effort to rebuild credibility after 2024. The current guide implies H2 2026 revenue of $2.68-2.75B, or roughly 9-11% growth over H2 2025, a deceleration from H1's 12-15% reported that the CFO attributed partly to a ~$15M FX headwind and tougher international comparisons; he also said Ireland-plant hiring would push Q3 gross margin to a peak followed by a step-down as the lines turn on ([Q2 2026 call transcript](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/investing/markets/markets-news/motley/3566565/dexcom-dxcm-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). The Q3 2026 release date had not been formally announced as of September 27; aggregators estimate October 22 or 29, and every prior Q3 release has been a late-October Thursday after the close ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/stocks/NASDAQ/DXCM/earnings/); [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/equities/dexcom-earnings)).

The reaction asymmetry deserves emphasis. Since July 2024 the stock has sold off on three beat-and-raise quarters (Q2 2025, Q3 2025 and, before the cut, Q1 2024) and its two largest rallies (Q1 2025, Q2 2026) coincided with margin bridges and capital-return announcements rather than revenue upside. Management commentary confirms the operational story behind the numbers: the July 2024 problems were commercial and self-inflicted; the 2025 problems (scrap, expedited freight, warning letter, recalls) were operational; and by Q2 2026 net promoter scores had risen for three consecutive quarters, complaints were down, and Q2 new customer starts matched the prior quarter's record with a sequential uptick in U.S. starts ([Q2 2026 call transcript](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/investing/markets/markets-news/motley/3566565/dexcom-dxcm-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). Q1 is structurally the weakest margin quarter (deductible resets), and non-GAAP operating margin in Q1 2023-2026 was 10.6%, 15.2%, 13.8% and 22.2%; the 840 bp jump in Q1 2026 is the clearest evidence that the 2024-2025 cost problems were fixed ([Q1 2026 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001093557/000109355726000072/dxcm3312026-exhibit991.htm)).

## Abbott is bigger but slowing, and Medicare is the swing factor

Abbott's FreeStyle Libre franchise is larger than Dexcom's globally and dominant internationally: Abbott's Q2 2026 Diabetes Care revenue was **$2.188B ($862M U.S., $1.326B international)**, its CGM sales grew 9.5% on a comparable basis, and Libre serves more than 7 million users, against Dexcom's roughly 3.5 million active customers ([PR Newswire, Abbott Q2 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/abbott-reports-second-quarter-2026-results-and-raises-full-year-eps-guidance-302827508.html); [Yahoo/Fierce JPM26](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/jpm26-dexcom-ceo-eyes-international-125140707.html)). Mordor puts global shipment share at Abbott 52.8%, Dexcom 33.9%, Medtronic 10.1%, and GlobalData put 2025 U.S. share at Dexcom 44.7% versus Abbott 48.5% ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/continuous-glucose-monitoring-market); [Medical Device Network](https://www.medicaldevice-network.com/news/dexcom-reports-2025-revenue-of-4-bn-yet-2026-outlook-lags-analyst-expectations/)). Those unit-share figures understate Dexcom's U.S. revenue position: its Q2 2026 U.S. revenue of $933M exceeded Abbott's entire U.S. Diabetes Care segment ($862M, which also includes blood-glucose meters), so Dexcom likely holds more than half of U.S. CGM revenue even at ~45% unit share, while internationally Abbott's segment is about 3.5x Dexcom's $375M. The more important 2026 fact is the growth crossover. Abbott's organic CGM growth ran 15-21% through 2025 and then slowed to **7.6% in Q1 2026 and 9.5% in Q2 2026**, partly on an international tender delay, while Dexcom held 12% organic in both quarters ([Abbott Q1 2026 release](https://abbott.mediaroom.com/2026-04-16-Abbott-Reports-First-Quarter-2026-Results-Updates-Guidance-to-Reflect-Acquisition-of-Exact-Sciences); [24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/04/17/abbot-price-prediction-where-will-the-stock-be-in-2027/)). In other words, Abbott took share in 2023-2025 (which is what Barclays's January 2026 Underweight was built on), and Dexcom appears to be taking it back in 2026 on G7 15 Day, record starts and non-insulin type 2 coverage; how much of that reversal is Abbott's one-off tender delay is not yet knowable.

The pump channel has become genuinely contested. Abbott's August 2024 deal made its sensor exclusive to Medtronic pumps, and Medtronic's MiniMed 780G with the Abbott-made Instinct sensor and the MiniMed Go smart-MDI system (FDA-cleared January 12, 2026) are now launched; Insulet rolled out Omnipod 5 with Libre 3 Plus in June 2026, and Tandem's t:slim X2 supports Libre 3 Plus in the U.S. and several European markets ([Medtronic](https://news.medtronic.com/2026-01-12-Medtronic-Diabetes-announces-FDA-clearance-for-MiniMed-Go-TM-Smart-MDI-system-featuring-Instinct-sensor-made-by-Abbott); [Insulet](https://investor.insulet.com/news/news-details/2026/Insulet-Announces-U-S--Rollout-of-Enhanced-Omnipod-5-Algorithm-and-Expands-Compatibility-with-Abbotts-FreeStyle-Libre-3-Plus-Sensor/default.aspx); [Tandem](https://investor.tandemdiabetes.com/news-releases/news-release-details/tandem-diabetes-care-launches-tslim-x2-insulin-pump)). Every major automated-insulin-delivery system now supports or will support a Libre sensor, so Dexcom's historical exclusivity in that channel is gone; its remaining advantages are incumbency and having the 15-day sensor integrated with Omnipod 5 and Tandem today. Abbott also won FDA De Novo authorization on August 25, 2026 for **Libre Duo, the first dual glucose-ketone sensor**, with U.S. rollout later this year and pump integrations targeted for 2026-2027; Dexcom's multi-analyte answer, G8 (half the size of G7, glucose then ketones and potassium), is slated for late 2027 or early 2028, giving Abbott a 12-18 month ketone exclusivity window most relevant to type 1 and SGLT2 users ([Abbott](https://abbott.mediaroom.com/2026-08-25-Abbott-receives-FDA-authorization-for-worlds-first-dual-glucose-ketone-sensing-technology-for-people-with-diabetes); [AOL Q2 2026 transcript](https://www.aol.com/articles/dexcom-dxcm-q2-2026-earnings-001522000.html)). Secondary competitors are not yet material: MiniMed (IPO February 2026, ~$2.7B revenue, net loss) sells Simplera mainly to its own base and now also carries Abbott's sensor; Roche's SmartGuide and Senseonics's Eversense 365 (2026 guide $60-64M) are niche; no FDA-cleared non-invasive smartwatch sensor exists ([MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/medtronics-minimed-prices-ipo-at-up-to-784m/812972/); [Senseonics](https://www.senseonics.com/investor-relations/news-releases/2026/05-07-2026-210239056); [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/05/26/apple-watch-non-invasive-blood-sugar-plans/)). The structural pricing threat is international, where Chinese makers (Sinocare, Sibionics, Medtrum) sell at 30-50% discounts, Sibionics lists at roughly $30-60 a month in Europe, and Medtrum is reimbursed in more than 20 European countries ([GlucoseIntel](https://www.glucoseintel.com/brands/emerging/sibionics-cgm/); [EIN Presswire](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/943913413/medtrum-to-preview-new-s10-cgm-at-easd-2026)). Dexcom does not disclose ASPs, but 2025 revenue growth of 16% trailing active-customer growth of "20%+" implies per-customer revenue fell roughly 3-5% on mix toward type 2, pharmacy rebates, ONE+/Flex tiers and Stelo; 2026 margin expansion shows that erosion is being absorbed by cost-per-sensor gains from 15 Day rather than reversing ([Yahoo/Fierce JPM26](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/jpm26-dexcom-ceo-eyes-international-125140707.html)).

Reimbursement cuts both ways and is the single largest swing factor in the scenarios below. On the positive side, commercial coverage for non-insulin type 2 patients is now in place across the four largest PBMs (more than 7 million lives), the 300-patient CONNECT randomized trial presented at ADA in June 2026 showed a **1.6% A1C improvement in the CGM arm (0.9% versus control) with 97% median wear**, and management's base case is a CMS coverage decision for non-insulin type 2 "before the end of this year" with a mid-2027 effective date; about 25 million Americans have non-insulin type 2 diabetes, roughly half of Medicare age, and Piper Sandler estimated the ruling could add more than 250 bp to top-line growth ([AOL Q2 2026 transcript](https://www.aol.com/articles/dexcom-dxcm-q2-2026-earnings-001522000.html); [MedTech Dive, Aug 27, 2026](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-abbott-await-medicare-coverage-expansion-for-cgms/828775/); [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/earnings/dexcom-earnings-up-next-can-g7-rollout-offset-abbott-competition-93CH-4503348)). At the May 2026 Investor Day management said U.S. covered lives of 15 million should double to ~30 million by 2027 (Medicare +12M, Medicaid +3M), and about 9 million Americans already have coverage but are not yet on CGM ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/healthcare/articles/dexcom-maps-g8-launch-coverage-130807892.html)). Note, though, that no CMS proposed decision memo had surfaced in the Medicare Coverage Database as of late September 2026, so the timeline rests entirely on management and Abbott statements ([CMS MCD](https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/ncacal-decision-memo.aspx?proposed=Y&ncaid=231&keyword=continuous+glucose+monitor&keywordType=starts&areaId=all&docType=NCA,CAL,NCD,MEDCAC,TA,MCD,6,3,5,1,F,P&contractOption=all&sortBy=relevance&bc=1)). On the negative side, the November 2025 DMEPOS final rule brings Class II CGMs into Medicare competitive bidding no later than January 1, 2028, as a monthly rental with a **$272.69/month bid ceiling and payment at the 75th percentile of winning bids**, with the bid window in late 2026 and contracts awarded in 2027 ([Applied Policy](https://www.appliedpolicy.com/cms-finalizes-rule-on-durable-medical-equipment-prosthetics-orthotics-and-supplies-dmepos-competitive-bidding-program-without-major-changes/)). About 15% of Dexcom's revenue is fee-for-service Medicare, Dexcom already sells on a monthly bundle, and the rule does not touch CGM dispensed through Part D pharmacy benefit (the channel Dexcom has been shifting toward since 2023), which limits the exposure; but the rule also removes the five-year replacement lock-in and lowers switching costs for Medicare patients, and neither Dexcom nor Abbott has quantified the impact ([MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/cms-diabetes-bidding-tandem-insulet-dexcom/757182/)). The GLP-1 debate has, on the available evidence, resolved in Dexcom's favor: CONNECT's largest relative A1C gain was in the GLP-1-only cohort, Dexcom's data show CGM use nearly quadrupling among non-insulin users after GLP-1 initiation, and no source found evidence of GLP-1s reducing CGM adoption, though the long-run risk that GLP-1s shrink the intensive-insulin pool remains unquantified ([Dexcom](https://www.dexcom.com/en-us/all-access/clinical-corner/benefits-of-glp-1-drugs); [AOL Q2 2026 transcript](https://www.aol.com/articles/dexcom-dxcm-q2-2026-earnings-001522000.html)).

## The quality file is thick but has stopped growing; governance is settled, with one flag

The FDA warning letter of March 4, 2025 remains the most consequential open regulatory item. It followed inspections of Mesa (June 2024) and San Diego (October-November 2024) and cited seven quality-system violations plus a premarket violation: Dexcom replaced the material in the sensor resistance layer, the initial equivalence study did not meet its primary endpoint, additional in-vivo studies showed the new material "performs worse for accuracy," and FDA deemed the change a major modification requiring a new 510(k) that Dexcom had not committed to file; Dexcom stopped distributing G7 sensors made with the new material but, per FDA, "did not address G6 sensors" ([FDA warning letter 700835](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/dexcom-inc-700835-03042025)). Dexcom's public position is that "no design changes were made" and that it qualified a second-source raw material developed since 2021 ([MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-rejects-claims-fda-warning-letter/743769/)). That framing is directly contradicted by FDA's finding, and the letter's status is unresolved in the public record: the FY2025 10-K still describes it only as "received," the Q2 2026 10-Q does not mention it, and no close-out letter, re-inspection or 2025-2026 Form 483 was found ([Dexcom 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355726000027/dxcm-20251231.htm); [Dexcom 10-Q Q2 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001093557/000109355726000143/dxcm-20260630.htm)). The letter did not stop FDA from clearing G7 15 Day five weeks later, which supports management's claim that it does not restrict new submissions. The 2025 recall record is heavy: a Class I receiver-speaker recall (602,445 G7 receivers, ~704,000 across models, at least 56 injuries, zero deaths), a Class I G7 iOS/ONE+ app recall (907,842 units) for a missing "Sensor Failed" alert, and a Class I G6 Android app recall (333 units), followed in 2026 by a Class II watchOS/ONE iOS alert-delay recall (58,582 units) and a June 2026 correction for two scrapped G7 lots that were stolen during destruction and resold ([FDA G7 receiver](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfres/res.cfm?id=213508); [FDA app recall](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfres/res.cfm?id=215006); [FDA watchOS](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfRes/res.cfm?ID=219289); [UPMC notice](https://www.upmchealthplan.com/providers/medical/announcements-updates/announcements/2026-announcements/2026-06-voluntary-nationwide-recall-dexcom-g7-sensors-june-2026)). Notably, no recall has ever been tied to the resistance-layer accuracy issue itself; FDA handled it through the warning letter. Hunterbrook's September and October 2025 reports (with a disclosed short position) added the December 2023 date for the coating switch, complaint-rate statistics and 13 MAUDE-reported G7 deaths since launch; the FDA letter independently corroborates the core claim of an uncleared, accuracy-reducing change, but the date, statistics and causal links to deaths remain the short seller's allegations, and a Dexcom-funded clinician's peer-reviewed letter criticized the report's methodology ([Hunterbrook](https://hntrbrk.com/dexcom2/); [Forlenza, Diabetes Technology & Therapeutics](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/15209156251390814)). Management's rebuttal has come only through earnings calls: complaint rates "largely stable," accuracy complaints down, NPS up three quarters running ([MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-execs-fixed-g7-quality-problems/804383/)).

Litigation is extensive but entirely at the pleading stage. The 2024 guidance-cut securities case (S.D. Cal. 24-cv-1485, class period April 28, 2023 to July 25, 2024) has an amended complaint filed April 10, 2026 and a motion to dismiss filed June 9, 2026; a second securities track (class period January 8, 2024 to September 17, 2025) alleges unauthorized design changes; derivative suits are consolidated and stayed in federal court with additional suits in Delaware Chancery and S.D. Cal.; and five consumer G7 class actions were consolidated on May 15, 2026 as In re: Dexcom CGM Litigation. The company says it cannot estimate the outcome and no settlement, dismissal or verdict has been found ([Dexcom 10-Q Q2 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001093557/000109355726000143/dxcm-20260630.htm); [Labaton](https://www.labaton.com/news-insights/labaton-keller-sucharow-llp-announces-expanded-securities-class-action-lawsuit-filed-against-dexcom-inc-and-certain-executives)). The one legal item that is fully closed is the Abbott patent war: after a mixed March 2024 Delaware verdict (one patent infringed, not willful, no damages), the two companies signed a global settlement on December 20, 2024 with royalty-free cross-licenses, no payments either way and a covenant not to sue until December 20, 2034 ([Dexcom 8-K, Dec 20, 2024](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355724000197/dxcm-20241220.htm); [Law360](https://www.law360.com/articles/1816415/jury-says-dexcom-infringed-1-abbott-patent-in-mixed-verdict)). One note in this research cites a Zacks piece dating that settlement to June 2026; the 8-K is primary and December 20, 2024 is correct.

Governance has moved from disruption to consolidation. Kevin Sayer (CEO since 2015) notified the board on July 25, 2025 of his retirement effective January 1, 2026; took medical leave on September 14, 2025 with Jake Leach as interim principal executive officer and Mark Foletta as interim chair; and returned as Executive Chairman in March 2026. Leach, a 21-year Dexcom veteran and former CTO and COO, became President and CEO on January 1, 2026 with a $1.15M base, 150% target bonus and $15M of RSUs/PSUs; CFO Jereme Sylvain added the COO title effective September 14, 2026 ([Dexcom 8-K, July 25, 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355725000241/dxcm-20250725.htm); [Dexcom 8-K, Dec 19, 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355725000291/dxcm-20251219.htm); [Dexcom 8-K, Sept 10, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001093557/000109355726000175/dxcm-20260910.htm)). Chief Commercial Officer Teri Lawver retired at end-2024 after the sales-force restructuring and was replaced by Jon Coleman (ex-Masimo) in March 2025 ([MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-chief-commercial-officer-retire-earnings/731135/)). The May 27, 2026 annual meeting re-elected all 12 directors and passed say-on-pay with about 88% support; 2025 pay was $16.3M for Sayer and ~$8.0M for Leach ([Dexcom 8-K, May 27, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001093557/000109355726000105/dxcm-20260527.htm); [Medical Design & Outsourcing](https://www.medicaldesignandoutsourcing.com/dexcom-ceo-pay-executive-compensation-median-employee/)). Insider activity is routine: Sayer is selling under a February 2026 10b5-1 plan covering 107,027 shares, executives sold roughly $5.7-8.4M over the past 90 days at rising prices, and the only insider purchase found was Leach's late-2025 buy as interim CEO ([Kalkine](https://kalkinemedia.com/us/news/announcements/dexcom-executive-chair-kevin-r-sayer-disposes-of-26756-shares-via-prearranged-rule-10b5-1-plan); [MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/stocks/NASDAQ/DXCM/forecast/); [Simply Wall St](https://simplywall.st/stocks/us/healthcare/nasdaq-dxcm/dexcom/news/interim-ceo-of-dexcom-jacob-leach-buys-66-more-shares/amp)). ## Conclusion

The three-year story is not that Dexcom's business deteriorated but that the market stopped paying for a growth rate the company could no longer deliver, and then discounted a cluster of execution and quality failures that management has, on the evidence of the last three quarters, fixed. The distinction matters for what comes next. Earnings power has already been re-established: TTM GAAP EPS of $2.53 is 178% above the level of September 2023, FCF exceeds $1.4B, and the buyback is retiring 3-4% of the shares a year. What has not been re-established is a premium multiple, and it should not be: at 11-13% growth Dexcom is a mid-20s-to-low-30s forward P/E company, not a 70-90x one, and the historical averages are an artifact of years when GAAP earnings were negligible. The implication is that the stock's next 12-24 months will be decided less by whether Dexcom beats its own guidance (it now does so reliably) and more by two external verdicts: whether CMS extends Medicare coverage to non-insulin type 2 patients on the timeline management assumes, and whether Abbott's 2026 slowdown was a tender-timing blip or the start of Dexcom taking back share.

A second, less obvious takeaway is that Dexcom's earnings-day behavior has inverted. In 2023 the stock rallied on revenue beats; since mid-2024 it has rallied on margin bridges and buybacks and sold off on beat-and-raise quarters whenever gross margin or the next year's growth framing disappointed. That pattern, combined with the management-flagged Q3 gross-margin peak and step-down, means the late-October print is a genuine two-sided event even though the full-year guide is likely safe. Readers who want to size the risk should weight the unresolved items that no source could close: the FDA warning letter's status, Elliott's actual stake and its two directors, and the absence of any CMS proposed decision memo in the public database as of late September 2026. Those three gaps are also where the next re-rating, in either direction, is most likely to originate.
