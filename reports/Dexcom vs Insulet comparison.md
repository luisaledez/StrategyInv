# Insulet's crash inverts its premium to Dexcom

*Research date: September 27, 2026. This is general equity research for an informed reader, not personalized investment advice. Prices and GAAP financials come first from the local SEC EDGAR and Yahoo price cache in this repository (`turnaround/cache/{edgar,fundamentals,valuation,prices}/{PODD,DXCM}.*`, summarized in [local cache, PODD and DXCM](<C:/Users/lledezma/MyProjects/StrategiesInv/research_notes/Dexcom vs Insulet comparison/local_cache_podd_and_dxcm_data.md>) and [local cache, DXCM](<C:/Users/lledezma/MyProjects/StrategiesInv/research_notes/Dexcom DXCM deep dive/local_cache_price_and_ttm_data.md>)); non-GAAP figures come from each company's 8-K press releases on EDGAR; consensus, analyst and ownership data come from third-party aggregators and are labeled as such. The Dexcom half of the comparison builds on the September 27, 2026 Dexcom deep dive, whose scenarios are reproduced here so this report stands on its own.*

Insulet closed at **$136.13 on September 25, 2026, down 61% from its $352.82 all-time high of September 9, 2025 and 12% below where it stood three years ago**, while Dexcom closed at **$86.62, flat (+0.7%) over the same three years** after its own 61% crash and 58% recovery ([local cache, PODD and DXCM](<C:/Users/lledezma/MyProjects/StrategiesInv/research_notes/Dexcom vs Insulet comparison/local_cache_podd_and_dxcm_data.md>)). Neither stock's fundamentals went backwards: Insulet's trailing adjusted EPS rose roughly 3.6x over three years and 38% over the last twelve months, and Dexcom's trailing GAAP EPS rose 178%; both round trips are multiple compression, 76% for Insulet and 64% for Dexcom, triggered in each case by a single guidance event that destroyed the credibility of a 20%-plus growth multiple (Dexcom's July 2024 cut, Insulet's August 5, 2026 cut). The result is an inverted relationship. Through 2025 Insulet traded at a premium to Dexcom because it grew 30% against 15%; today Insulet grows 23% (guided to a mid-teens exit rate) against Dexcom's 12-13% organic, yet trades at **17.7x FY2027E earnings and 2.6x FY2027E sales against Dexcom's 27.8x and 5.5x**, a 36% P/E discount for the faster grower ([local cache, PODD and DXCM](<C:/Users/lledezma/MyProjects/StrategiesInv/research_notes/Dexcom vs Insulet comparison/local_cache_podd_and_dxcm_data.md>); [StockAnalysis PODD](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/podd/statistics/); [StockAnalysis DXCM](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/dxcm/statistics/)). The discount is not free: Insulet's growth is now lower-quality (Type 2 patients churn at more than double the Type 1 rate in their first 90 days, three tubeless competitors launch into its pharmacy channel between late 2026 and mid-2027, two Class I pod recalls and a securities class action landed in 2026), it converts only 9% of revenue to free cash flow against Dexcom's 28%, it carries $414M of net debt against Dexcom's $549M of net cash, and its short interest has tripled to 7.4% of float. On a 12-24 month view, Insulet's scenarios (bear $90-120 at 30%, base $155-185 at 50%, bull $215-260 at 20%) give a probability-weighted value of about **$164, or +20%**, against Dexcom's **$95, or +10%** (bear $55-70 at 25%, base $90-105 at 50%, bull $110-135 at 25%). Insulet therefore offers the better expected return and the more asymmetric payoff, with a bear case that is a smaller percentage loss than Dexcom's precisely because its multiple has already collapsed, but Dexcom offers the higher-confidence return: proven margin recovery, a 4.3% FCF yield, a 2.6% buyback yield, and a bull case that depends on an external CMS decision rather than on a commercial fix that Insulet has not yet demonstrated. The choice between them is a choice about which risk you would rather own for the next two quarters: Insulet's late-October print, which will either confirm the mid-teens exit rate or cut again, or Dexcom's, which carries a management-flagged gross-margin step-down into a multiple that has already re-rated.

## Side by side: Insulet grows faster, Dexcom earns more, and the multiples have swapped

The two companies are very different in scale and cash generation despite their shared patient base. Dexcom is 3.5x Insulet by market capitalization ($32.7B versus $9.44B) and 1.6x by trailing revenue ($4.97B versus $3.05B), but 5.3x by free cash flow ($1,405M versus $266M), because Dexcom converts 28% of revenue to FCF and Insulet only 9% ([local cache, PODD and DXCM](<C:/Users/lledezma/MyProjects/StrategiesInv/research_notes/Dexcom vs Insulet comparison/local_cache_podd_and_dxcm_data.md>)). Part of that gap is structural (Insulet spends 42% of revenue on SG&A, including direct-to-consumer advertising and a rapidly expanded field force, and capex is climbing with the Malaysia and Costa Rica plants) and part is transitory (Insulet's TTM FCF is depressed by $60-70M of device-correction costs and a capex step-up from $30.9M to $56.8M in H1 2026 versus H1 2025) ([Insulet Q2 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001145197/000114519726000169/podd-20260630.htm)). Insulet's gross margin is structurally higher (70-73% versus 63-64%) but its operating margin is lower (16-19% versus 22-25%), because the pod is a manufactured consumable sold through wholesalers and pharmacies whereas the sensor business has passed through its heavy investment phase and now leverages a fixed cost base.

### The historical relationship has fully inverted

Through 2025 the two stocks carried the same five-year median trailing multiple (83x) but Insulet traded at a large premium at any given moment because it was growing twice as fast: in September-October 2025 Insulet was at roughly 83-104x trailing earnings and 12-13x EV/Sales against Dexcom's 47x and 6-7x ([local cache, PODD and DXCM](<C:/Users/lledezma/MyProjects/StrategiesInv/research_notes/Dexcom vs Insulet comparison/local_cache_podd_and_dxcm_data.md>); [Seeking Alpha PODD valuation metrics](https://seekingalpha.com/symbol/PODD/valuation/metrics)). Insulet's EV/Sales averaged 12.7x over five years and its forward P/E ranged roughly 35-70x over 2021-2025; Dexcom's three-, five- and ten-year average trailing P/Es were 53x, 71x and 58x ([FullRatio DXCM](https://fullratio.com/stocks/nasdaq-dxcm/pe-ratio); [Wisesheets PODD](https://www.wisesheets.io/pe-ratio/PODD)). Both histories are inflated by years when GAAP earnings were negligible (Insulet's P/E exceeded 900x in 2019-2022), so neither company's historical average is a credible re-rating anchor. What matters is the relative position today: a Motley Fool comparison at June 4, 2026 already showed Insulet at 22.0x forward against Dexcom's 28.4x with 30.9% versus 15.6% FY2025 growth, and the gap has since widened to 17.7x versus 27.8x on FY2027E even as the growth gap has narrowed to roughly three points (13.8% versus 10.9% consensus) ([Motley Fool, DexCom vs. Insulet](https://www.fool.com/coverage/better-buy/2026/06/04/dexcom-vs-insulet-which-diabetes-stock-is-a-better-buy-in-2026/)). The market is pricing Insulet's growth as lower-quality rather than merely lower, and the rest of this report is about whether that judgment is right.

## Two round trips: Dexcom crashed on a guidance cut in 2024, Insulet on one in 2026

Both three-year charts are the same story told two years apart: a premium multiple, a beat-and-raise streak that made the multiple feel safe, and then a single guidance event that removed the growth assumption and took 40% (Dexcom, one day) or 61% (Insulet, over eleven months) off the price. The difference is timing. Dexcom's crash came in July 2024 and its recovery is eighteen months old; Insulet's came in 2026 and is seven weeks old. That is why Dexcom is flat and Insulet is down 12% over three years despite Insulet's earnings compounding faster.

### Dexcom's four legs, briefly

Dexcom slid from $128.51 to $93.30 in the summer-2023 GLP-1 scare, rallied to a cycle high of **$140.45 on April 9, 2024** (about 91x trailing GAAP EPS), then fell **40.7% on July 26, 2024** after a Q2 revenue miss and a FY2024 guidance cut from $4.20-4.35B to $4.00-4.05B caused by a sales-force realignment, faster G7 rebate eligibility and DME channel share loss ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/07/26/dexcom-shares-plunge-more-than-40percent-after-q2-earnings.html); [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-q2-earnings-sales-forecast-shortfall/722540/)). A fifteen-month grind followed on margin and quality rather than revenue: Q4 2024 non-GAAP gross margin fell to 59.4%, an FDA warning letter (March 4, 2025) cited an uncleared sensor-material change, the FY2025 gross-margin guide was cut twice, three Class I recalls landed, CEO Kevin Sayer took medical leave and Hunterbrook published a short report on the G7 coating (September 2025), and the Q3 2025 call framed 2026 as "slightly below" the Street, producing a 14.6% drop and a **trough of $54.84 on November 10, 2025** ([Dexcom 8-K, Mar 4, 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355725000047/dxcm-20250304.htm); [Hunterbrook](https://hntrbrk.com/dexcom/); [Investing.com Q3 2025 call](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-dexcom-beats-q3-2025-estimates-stock-steady-93CH-4322136)). The recovery from there rests on three consecutive beat-and-raise quarters, non-GAAP gross margin back to 64.1%, record operating margin, a May 2026 Investor Day with 2030 targets and a $1B buyback, and a 12.0% jump on July 31, 2026 after Q2; the stock peaked at $92.34 on August 21 before settling at $86.62 ([Dexcom Q2 2026 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001093557/000109355726000142/dxcm06302026-exhibit991.htm); [local cache, DXCM](<C:/Users/lledezma/MyProjects/StrategiesInv/research_notes/Dexcom DXCM deep dive/local_cache_price_and_ttm_data.md>)).

### Insulet's three legs: GLP-1 scare, a fourteen-quarter beat-and-raise streak, then the 2026 unwind

Insulet's 2023 low of **$127.77 on October 12, 2023** had nothing to do with the company: Novo Nordisk's SELECT cardiovascular data (August 8, 2023, -8.6% that day) and the early stop of the FLOW kidney trial (October 10, 2023, -8.7% the next day) convinced the market that GLP-1s would shrink insulin use, and Insulet's Q2 2023 beat-and-raise released the same evening as SELECT could not stop the slide ([Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4010020-insulet-tandem-drop-weight-loss-drugs-hurt-insulin-use); [Motley Fool, Oct 12, 2023](https://www.fool.com/investing/2023/10/12/why-shares-of-insulet-dropped-this-week)). The recovery to **$352.82 on September 9, 2025** was built on fourteen consecutive quarters of beating consensus and raising or initiating guidance that was then raised (Q3 2022 through Q1 2026), the FDA's August 26, 2024 clearance of Omnipod 5 for Type 2 (the first AID system for both types; +6.6% and +6.2% on August 27-28), the appointment of Ashley McEvoy as CEO on April 28, 2025, and two 20%-plus and 9%-plus post-earnings rallies in May and August 2025 as FY2025 guidance went from 16-20% to 28-29% ([Motley Fool, Aug 30, 2024](https://www.fool.com/investing/2024/08/30/why-insulet-stock-was-zooming-higher-this-week/); [Insulet Q1 2025 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1145197/000114519725000021/podd2025-03x31ex991.htm); [Insulet Q3 2025 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1145197/000114519725000067/podd2025-09x30ex991.htm)). At the peak the stock traded at roughly 83x trailing adjusted EPS.

The unwind came in four sequential legs, each removing one pillar of that multiple. The first was doubt about the long-range plan: on the November 20, 2025 Investor Day management committed to a ~20% revenue CAGR through 2028, ~100 bp a year of margin expansion and 25%-plus EPS growth, and the stock fell **9.7% the same day** as analysts judged Omnipod 6 "more software-driven and connectivity-focused" with no form-factor change while "at least two competitor patch pumps" would launch by 2027; Barclays then downgraded both Insulet and Dexcom to Underweight on January 12, 2026 on the grounds that "intensifying competition will be the dominant factor" ([Insulet Investor Day release](https://investors.insulet.com/news/news-details/2025/Insulet-Outlines-Long-Term-Strategy-to-Drive-Growth-and-Value-Creation-at-2025-Investor-Day/default.aspx); [MedTech Dive, Nov 24, 2025](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/insulet-patch-pump-plans/806292/); [Investing.com, Barclays](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/barclays-downgrades-dexcom-insulet-as-diabetes-competition-seen-intensifying-in-2-4442462)). The second was product quality: a voluntary correction of Omnipod 5 pod lots on March 12, 2026 (tubing tears causing silent insulin under-delivery; -6.9% on March 13), its expansion on April 10, and on **April 29, 2026 the FDA's Class I classification with 29 serious injuries reported, which coincided with a 12.5% one-day drop**; a second correction on May 26 covered about 7 million Omnipod 5, DASH and Eros pods (8.5% of 2025 production, 24 serious injuries), and a securities class action (Hu v. Insulet, class period February 21, 2025 to May 26, 2026) followed on July 2 ([FDA recall notice, March 2026](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/medical-device-recalls-and-early-alerts/insulin-pump-recall-insulet-removes-certain-omnipod-5-pods); [FDA, May 2026 correction](https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/insulet-initiates-voluntary-medical-device-correction-certain-omnipodr-pods-us-and-affected); [Insulet Q2 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001145197/000114519726000169/podd-20260630.htm)). The third was growth quality: the Q1 2026 print on May 6 beat and raised the total-company guide to 21-23%, but left the US guide unchanged, embedded a 200 bp inventory headwind in Q2, disclosed a sequential decline in US new starts and "modest retention deterioration" as Type 2 became about 40% of starts, and the stock fell **9.7%** ([Motley Fool Q1 2026 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/05/06/insulet-podd-q1-2026-earnings-transcript/)). The fourth was the cut itself: on **August 5, 2026 the Q2 print beat on every line (revenue $801.7M, +23.5%; adjusted EPS $1.66 versus ~$1.46) but cut FY2026 US Omnipod growth to 17-19% from 20-22%, guided Q3 to 17.5-19.5% (US 14-16%) and framed 2027 as a "mid-teens" exit rate, and the stock fell 20.1%**, with at least six downgrades in 48 hours and target cuts of 25-50% (JPMorgan $275 to $152, TD Cowen $294 to $144, Wells Fargo $255 to $144, Leerink $247 to $145) ([Insulet Q2 2026 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001145197/000114519726000167/podd2026-06x30ex991.htm); [Motley Fool Q2 2026 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/12/insulet-podd-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/); [Investing.com, JPMorgan](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/jpmorgan-cuts-insulet-to-neutral-on-slowing-us-growth-trims-target-by-45-4841939); [MarketBeat PODD forecast](https://www.marketbeat.com/stocks/NASDAQ/PODD/forecast/)). CEO McEvoy's words on the call were unusually direct: "We are seeing lower rates of utilization and retention among type 2 customers, and these trends were more pronounced this quarter than we anticipated," and "I should have identified the issue sooner" ([Motley Fool Q2 2026 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/12/insulet-podd-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). September added an 8.4% drift lower with the stock now at $136.13, roughly its October 2023 GLP-1-scare low, on earnings 3.6x higher ([local cache, PODD and DXCM](<C:/Users/lledezma/MyProjects/StrategiesInv/research_notes/Dexcom vs Insulet comparison/local_cache_podd_and_dxcm_data.md>)).

**Conflict flag on the April 29, 2026 move.** No contemporaneous article explicitly attributes the 12.5% close-to-close drop to a single cause. The best-sourced explanation is the FDA's same-day Class I classification of the March correction and an Insulet 8-K filed that day disclosing that FDA's database listed 476 serious-injury medical-device reports against 29 confirmed serious adverse events; Insulet re-alerted customers the same day and MarketBeat logged an 8.9% intraday drop ([FDA recall notice](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/medical-device-recalls-and-early-alerts/insulin-pump-recall-insulet-removes-certain-omnipod-5-pods); [Insulet Apr 29, 2026 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001145197/000114519726000095/podd-20260429.htm); [MarketBeat, Apr 29, 2026](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/insulet-nasdaqpodd-shares-down-89-heres-what-happened-2026-04-29/)). The alternatives are weaker: Rothschild Redburn's downgrade (Neutral, target $220 from $380, growth outlook cut to 11%) was five days earlier on April 24, and Dexcom's Q1 report came after the close on April 30, so neither fits the date ([Investing.com, Rothschild](https://www.investing.com/news/analyst-ratings/rothschild-downgrades-insulet-stock-rating-on-competition-concerns-93CH-4634808)). The date match with the FDA classification and the 8-K is exact and the sources are primary, so that attribution is preferred, with the caveat that it is inferred rather than reported.

From the September 2025 peak Insulet's EPS rose 38% while the multiple fell 72%, which is the arithmetic of a 61% decline; from its own April 2024 peak Dexcom's EPS rose 64% while its multiple fell 63%. The two compressions differ in one important way. Dexcom's happened in stages over eighteen months and has partially reversed (its forward multiple is up roughly 20-25% from the November 2025 trough, and most of the 58% rebound is earnings growth rather than re-rating), whereas Insulet's has happened almost entirely within twelve months (trailing P/E 56.6x in June 2025, 82.9x in December 2025, 60.3x in March 2026, 35.6x in June 2026, 24.8x in September 2026) and has not yet begun to reverse ([local cache, PODD and DXCM](<C:/Users/lledezma/MyProjects/StrategiesInv/research_notes/Dexcom vs Insulet comparison/local_cache_podd_and_dxcm_data.md>)). The reason both compressions were so violent is the same: at 80-95x, the multiple was pricing durable 20%-plus growth, so a guide that implied mid-teens growth did not cost a few turns, it cost the entire premium. Rothschild framed Insulet's case explicitly in April 2026: at ~55x, "consensus expectations for share gains are at odds with the evolving commercial and competitive landscape," and it cut its growth outlook from ~19% (FY2025-2028) to 11% (FY2027-2030) ([Investing.com, Rothschild](https://www.investing.com/news/analyst-ratings/rothschild-downgrades-insulet-stock-rating-on-competition-concerns-93CH-4634808)).

## Sixteen quarters: Insulet beat 31 of 32 consensus numbers and still lost 61%

Insulet's operating record over the four years is close to flawless on the numbers the sell side tracks. It beat consensus revenue in all sixteen quarters, beat consensus adjusted EPS in fifteen (the exception being Q2 2024, $0.55 versus ~$0.56), raised the current-year revenue guide at every non-year-initiating report from Q3 2022 through Q1 2026 (the Q4 reports initiated the next year's range, which was then raised at each of the following three reports), and cut for the first time at Q2 2026. Dexcom's record is slightly less clean: fifteen revenue beats (miss: Q2 2024), fourteen EPS beats (misses: Q4 2024 and Q1 2025), one revenue-guide cut (July 2024) and two gross-margin-guide cuts (2025). Yet the market punished four of Dexcom's beat quarters with 5-15% drops and Insulet's last two beats with drops of 9.7% and 20.1%, because in both cases the stock was pricing the trajectory of next year's growth, not the quarter.

Three patterns stand out. First, the growth gap was widest in 2024-2025 (Insulet +17-38% against Dexcom +2-27%) and is narrowing fast: Insulet's guided Q3 2026 range of 17.5-19.5% cc and Q4 exit of 12-17% cc converge on Dexcom's 12-13% organic ([Insulet Q2 2026 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001145197/000114519726000167/podd2026-06x30ex991.htm); [Investing.com, Wells Fargo conference](https://ng.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/insulet-at-wells-fargo-conference-guidance-cut-meets-longterm-optimism-93CH-2690101)). Second, the margin stories run in opposite directions: Insulet's gross margin has been stable at 70-73% since Q4 2023 and its adjusted operating margin has expanded from 12% to 19% on schedule, while Dexcom's gross margin fell from 67% to 57.5% and has climbed back to 64%, so Dexcom's margin recovery is the driver of its 2026 estimate upgrades (FY2026 EPS revisions were 23 up and none down over 30 days), whereas Insulet's margin expansion is already in the numbers ([Yahoo DXCM analysis](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/DXCM/analysis/)). Third, Insulet's H1 2026 gross margin absorbed about 90 bp of warranty and obsolescence costs from the device corrections while still expanding adjusted operating margin 110-140 bp, which is the strongest evidence that the cost structure is intact and that the problem is confined to the top line ([Insulet Q2 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001145197/000114519726000169/podd-20260630.htm)).

## Pods versus sensors: Insulet sells a $370-a-month consumable to a churn-prone new patient, Dexcom a far cheaper sensor to a far larger pool

The two businesses are complementary at the patient level and very different at the unit-economics level. Dexcom sells a sensor worn by roughly 3.5 million people, insulin users and increasingly non-insulin Type 2 patients, at a per-customer revenue that has been drifting down 3-5% a year on mix and rebates while cost per sensor falls faster; Insulet sells a disposable three-day pod to more than 600,000 people, almost all insulin-intensive, at roughly $4,400-4,800 per user per year (an inference from FY2025 Omnipod revenue of $2.674B over the year-end base) with pods listing at about $820 per ten-pod box ([Yahoo/Fierce JPM26](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/jpm26-dexcom-ceo-eyes-international-125140707.html); [Insulet Q4 2025 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1145197/000114519726000026/podd202512-31ex991.htm); [SingleCare](https://www.singlecare.com/blog/omnipod-without-insurance/)). Every Insulet customer is a CGM customer, but only a small fraction of CGM customers are pump customers, so Dexcom's addressable pool is an order of magnitude larger (25 million non-insulin Type 2 Americans alone) while Insulet's is deeper per patient. That asymmetry defines how each company's Type 2 push works.

### The Type 2 opportunity: incremental sensor volume for Dexcom, an onboarding problem for Insulet

Dexcom's Type 2 expansion is low-risk by construction. Commercial coverage for non-insulin Type 2 patients is in place across the four largest PBMs (more than 7 million lives), the 300-patient CONNECT randomized trial presented at ADA 2026 showed a 0.9-point A1C improvement versus control with 97% median wear, and management's base case is a CMS coverage decision for non-insulin Type 2 "before the end of this year" with a mid-2027 effective date that would roughly double US covered lives from 15 million to 30 million; Piper Sandler estimated the ruling could add more than 250 bp to growth ([AOL Q2 2026 transcript](https://www.aol.com/articles/dexcom-dxcm-q2-2026-earnings-001522000.html); [MedTech Dive, Aug 27, 2026](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-abbott-await-medicare-coverage-expansion-for-cgms/828775/); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/healthcare/articles/dexcom-maps-g8-launch-coverage-130807892.html)). A Type 2 patient who stops wearing a sensor costs Dexcom a stream of roughly $1,400 a year (TTM revenue over ~3.5 million customers, an approximation) and little else; no CMS proposed decision memo had appeared in the Medicare Coverage Database as of late September, so the timeline rests on management's statements ([CMS MCD](https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/ncacal-decision-memo.aspx?proposed=Y&ncaid=231&keyword=continuous+glucose+monitor&keywordType=starts&areaId=all&docType=NCA,CAL,NCD,MEDCAC,TA,MCD,6,3,5,1,F,P&contractOption=all&sortBy=relevance&bc=1)).

Insulet's Type 2 push is the same thesis with the opposite risk profile. Omnipod 5 was cleared for Type 2 adults in August 2024 on SECURE-T2D data (A1c from 8.2% to 7.4%, about five more hours a day in range), Type 2 rose from over 30% of US new starts in Q1 2025 to over 40% in Q2 2026, and the 2026 ADA Standards of Care made AID the preferred insulin-delivery method for Type 2 for the first time ([Insulet SECURE-T2D release](https://investors.insulet.com/news/news-details/2024/Insulets-SECURE-T2D-Pivotal-Trial-Results-Demonstrate-Omnipod-5-Improves-Clinical-Outcomes-and-Quality-of-Life-in-Type-2-Diabetes/default.aspx); [Motley Fool Q2 2026 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/12/insulet-podd-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/); [Omnipod ADA 2026](https://www.omnipod.com/hcp/blog/tips-guidelines/2026-ada-standards-of-care)). But the Type 2 patient Insulet is onboarding is structurally different from its Type 1 base: about 60% Medicare/Medicaid (against ~40% for Type 1), more comorbidities, slower disease progression, prescribed by primary-care physicians rather than endocrinologists, and reached increasingly through direct-to-consumer advertising (about 25% of starts) ([BigGo Finance, Q2 2026 call](https://finance.biggo.com/news/US_PODD_2026-08-05); [Drug Delivery Business, Investor Day](https://www.drugdeliverybusiness.com/insulet-ceo-ashley-mcevoy-omnipod-6-innovation-roadmap/)). Management says Type 2 attrition is "more than double" the ~10% Type 1 rate but rejects analyst estimates of 40%; the sell side models 20-30% for insulin-intensive Type 2 and over 50% for non-intensive (Oppenheimer), or 30-50% blended Type 2 with US blended attrition at 17.5% versus 10% previously (Wells Fargo) ([Investing.com, Wells Fargo conference](https://ng.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/insulet-at-wells-fargo-conference-guidance-cut-meets-longterm-optimism-93CH-2690101); [Investing.com, Oppenheimer](https://ca.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/oppenheimer-downgrades-insulet-stock-rating-on-diabetes-growth-concerns-93CH-4778851); [Investing.com, Wells Fargo](https://in.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/wells-fargo-cuts-insulet-stock-rating-on-attrition-concerns-93CH-5538622)). The drop-off is concentrated in the first 90 days (first pod change, first refill, insurance re-authorization) and retention stabilizes after that, which is why the fix is commercial rather than clinical: sales compensation re-weighted to 45-day retention rather than starts, expanded insurance-navigation and onboarding teams, more targeted sampling, and the Omnipod Discover cloud platform (12,000-plus patients, 1,600 providers) ([Motley Fool Q2 2026 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/12/insulet-podd-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). Two further points matter for judging it. The corrections hit exactly the onboarding window (pod changes and refills) in H1 2026, so some of the "retention" shortfall is plausibly recall-related even though management did not attribute it that way; and Tandem, whose Type 2 starts come mostly through endocrinologists, reports Type 2 attrition "only modestly higher" than Type 1, which suggests the problem is Insulet's primary-care and Medicaid-heavy funnel rather than Type 2 patients as a class ([Motley Fool, Tandem Q2 2026 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/13/tandem-diabetes-tndm-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). Management is explicit that the 2027 framework "does not assume any benefit" from the retention actions, so any improvement is upside to the mid-teens exit rate ([Investing.com Q2 2026 transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-insulet-q2-2026-beat-overshadowed-by-guidance-cut-93CH-4837747)). What neither company discloses is the number that would settle the debate: Insulet does not publish Type 2 active users, retention percentages or utilization, and no third party has them.

### Competition: Abbott is one giant rival, Insulet faces three new patch pumps at once

Dexcom's competitive problem is a single, larger, slowing rival. Abbott's Libre franchise serves more than 7 million users and its Q2 2026 Diabetes Care revenue was $2.188B ($862M US, $1.326B international), against Dexcom's Q2 revenue of $1.308B ($933M US), so Dexcom likely holds more than half of US CGM revenue at roughly 45% unit share while Abbott is about 3.5x larger internationally ([PR Newswire, Abbott Q2 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/abbott-reports-second-quarter-2026-results-and-raises-full-year-eps-guidance-302827508.html); [Medical Device Network](https://www.medicaldevice-network.com/news/dexcom-reports-2025-revenue-of-4-bn-yet-2026-outlook-lags-analyst-expectations/)). The 2026 growth crossover is the important fact: Abbott's organic CGM growth slowed from 15-21% through 2025 to 7.6% and 9.5% in Q1 and Q2 2026 (partly an international tender delay) while Dexcom held 12% organic, and Barclays's January Underweight was built on the opposite trend ([Abbott Q1 2026 release](https://abbott.mediaroom.com/2026-04-16-Abbott-Reports-First-Quarter-2026-Results-Updates-Guidance-to-Reflect-Acquisition-of-Exact-Sciences)). Abbott's two structural advantages are its exclusive sensor deal with MiniMed (Instinct sensor launched with MiniMed in Europe in July and the US in August 2026, with an exclusive glucose-ketone integration in 2027) and its August 25, 2026 De Novo authorization for Libre Duo, the first dual glucose-ketone sensor, which gives it a 12-18 month ketone window until Dexcom's G8 in late 2027 or early 2028 ([MiniMed 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002062583/000162828026060926/mdt-20260731.htm); [Abbott Libre Duo](https://abbott.mediaroom.com/2026-08-25-Abbott-receives-FDA-authorization-for-worlds-first-dual-glucose-ketone-sensing-technology-for-people-with-diabetes)). Chinese makers (Sinocare, Sibionics, Medtrum) price 30-50% below Dexcom in Europe but have no US presence ([GlucoseIntel](https://www.glucoseintel.com/brands/emerging/sibionics-cgm/)).

Insulet's competitive problem is newer and more concentrated. It is, on the evidence, still the largest source of US new pump starts (record starts in Q4 2025 and FY2025, the second-highest quarter ever in Q2 2026, a customer base growing 23%, #1 in new starts in the US and Europe per the company, and "dominated new device starts" per Leerink's Mike Kratky), against Tandem shipping 22,000 US pumps in Q2 2026 (+7%), MiniMed selling 34,000 new pumps globally in the quarter to July 31, and Beta Bionics growing starts 10-20% sequentially off $32M of quarterly revenue ([Insulet Q4 2025 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1145197/000114519726000026/podd202512-31ex991.htm); [MedTech Dive, Beta Bionics Mint](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/beta-bionics-insulin-patch-pump-gets-fda-nod/830332/); [Motley Fool, Tandem Q2 2026](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/13/tandem-diabetes-tndm-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/); [MiniMed 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002062583/000162828026060926/mdt-20260731.htm)). But Insulet's tubeless monopoly ends within the next nine months. Tandem filed a 510(k) for a tubeless AID system on its Mobi hardware in Q2 2026 with a scaled launch planned for late 2026 and is already running pay-as-you-go pharmacy pricing at a ~$350-a-month net price with contracts covering ~80% of PBM lives; Beta Bionics received FDA clearance for its Mint patch pump in September 2026 (tubeless, three-day wear, 200-unit reservoir, both Abbott and Dexcom sensors) with a full US launch in Q1 2027 exclusively through pharmacy and a plan for "at least 1.5 million" disposable units in 2027; MiniMed submitted its Fit patch pump to FDA in September 2026 with full US launch expected summer 2027; Sequel's twiist is on the market with Type 2 data; and the Federal Circuit's May 2026 reversal of Insulet's EOFlow verdict vacated the worldwide injunction that kept the EOPatch out of key markets ([Motley Fool, Tandem Q2 2026](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/13/tandem-diabetes-tndm-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/); [Yahoo Finance, Tandem pharmacy roadmap](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/healthcare/articles/tandem-diabetes-care-maps-pharmacy-180156337.html); [MedTech Dive, Beta Bionics Mint](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/beta-bionics-insulin-patch-pump-gets-fda-nod/830332/); [MedTech Dive, MiniMed Fit](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/minimed-submits-new-patch-pump-for-fda-clearance/829313/); [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/coverage/2026/2026-05-28-eoflow-wins-federal-circuit-appeal-overturning-$452-million-jury-verdict)). Insulet's 2027 framework "incorporates expected competitive entrants" and "stable pricing" but "does not model market share losses," and CFO Flavia Pease has said Insulet is "not taking any positions that would result in exclusionary positions in access" after a Blue plan reportedly rolled back pharmacy access for tubed competitors ([Investing.com Q2 2026 transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-insulet-q2-2026-beat-overshadowed-by-guidance-cut-93CH-4837747); [BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/US_PODD_2026-08-05)). Tandem's $350-a-month anchor is close to Insulet's implied $370-400 blended revenue per user, so a price war is not yet visible; 2027 pod pricing is nonetheless the main margin risk. On balance, Dexcom's competitive picture is improving at the margin (a slowing rival, share recapture on G7 15 Day) while Insulet's is deteriorating at the margin (three simultaneous launches into a channel it has had to itself), and that difference is a legitimate part of the valuation gap.

### Channel and Medicare: Insulet's pharmacy model dodges competitive bidding, Dexcom is partly exposed

Insulet sells through the pharmacy channel with no upfront durable: 86% of global Omnipod sales go through intermediaries, three distributors were 27%, 26% and 25% of 2025 revenue, the majority of commercially insured Omnipod 5 users pay under $30 a month and Medicare covers it under Part D as a drug-delivery device ([Insulet FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1145197/000114519726000028/podd-20251231.htm); [Omnipod.com](https://www.omnipod.com/is-omnipod-right-for-me); [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/after-long-wait-omnipod-achieves-coverage-through-medicare-part-d)). The November 28, 2025 CMS final rule brings Class II CGMs and insulin pumps into Part B DMEPOS competitive bidding no later than January 1, 2028 as a bundled monthly rental (CGM bid ceiling $272.69 a month, payment at the 75th percentile of winning bids, the five-year replacement lock-in eliminated), with the bid window in late 2026 and contracts in 2027 ([MedTech Dive, final rule](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/cms-final-rule-diabetes-competitive-bidding/806719/); [Applied Policy](https://www.appliedpolicy.com/cms-finalizes-rule-on-durable-medical-equipment-prosthetics-orthotics-and-supplies-dmepos-competitive-bidding-program-without-major-changes/)). Insulet says Omnipod "may not qualify" for Part B bidding because it is sold pay-as-you-go through pharmacy, and RBC expects Tandem and Medtronic to be most affected and Insulet least; Tandem's pharmacy shift is partly a response ([MedTech Dive, Aug 8, 2025](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/cms-diabetes-bidding-tandem-insulet-dexcom/757182/)). Dexcom is more exposed: about 15% of its revenue is fee-for-service Medicare, it already bills as a monthly bundle and has been shifting to Part D pharmacy since 2023 (the rule does not touch Part D), but neither Dexcom nor Abbott has quantified the impact and the removal of the replacement lock-in lowers switching costs for Medicare patients. Insulet's exposure is indirect but real: about 60% of its Type 2 starts are Medicare/Medicaid, the 2026 Part D out-of-pocket cap is $2,100, many Part D plans are moving preferred brands from flat copays to coinsurance, and Oppenheimer calls "Part-D subsidy removal" a key watch point ([GoodRx](https://www.goodrx.com/omnipod-5/medicare-coverage); [Investing.com, Oppenheimer](https://ca.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/oppenheimer-downgrades-insulet-stock-rating-on-diabetes-growth-concerns-93CH-4778851)). Neither company has significant 2026 tariff exposure, but Insulet's 10-Q warns that losing the current medical-device exemption "would have a material impact" in future years ([Insulet Q2 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001145197/000114519726000169/podd-20260630.htm)).

### The sensor partnership: Insulet is now sensor-agnostic, and Dexcom has no captive pump partner left

Omnipod 5 integrates Dexcom G6, G7 and G7 15 Day and, since 2025-2026, Abbott's Libre 2 Plus and, in the US from June 3, 2026, Libre 3 Plus, alongside an enhanced algorithm with six glucose targets; the Omnipod 5 iPhone app with G7 integration reached all US users in June 2025 ([BusinessWire, June 3, 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260603466418/en/Insulet-Announces-U.S.-Rollout-of-Enhanced-Omnipod-5-Algorithm-and-Expands-Compatibility-with-Abbotts-FreeStyle-Libre-3-Plus-Sensor); [BusinessWire, June 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250610766006/en/Insulets-Omnipod-5-App-for-iPhone-with-Dexcom-G7-Integration-Now-Available-to-all-U.S.-Users)). Dexcom completed G7 15 Day integration with Tandem in Q2 2026 so that "our G7 15-day system is now accessible for all adult G7 customers in the US" and expects about half its US base on 15 Day by year-end 2026, but neither company discloses the Dexcom/Abbott split among Omnipod 5 users and no analyst estimate exists ([Motley Fool, Dexcom Q2 2026 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/07/30/dexcom-dxcm-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). Three implications follow. With over 95% of US Omnipod users on Omnipod 5 and more than 600,000 users globally, Insulet's installed base is a meaningful AID channel for Dexcom, so every Type 2 Omnipod start on a Dexcom sensor that churns in 90 days is also a lost Dexcom customer (an unquantified but real headwind). Libre 3 Plus availability on Omnipod 5 gives Medicare/Medicaid-heavy Type 2 patients a cheaper sensor and likely shifts incremental Omnipod sensor mix toward Abbott over time (inference; no data). And the industry has moved to multi-sensor integration rather than exclusivity: MiniMed is Abbott-exclusive, Beta Bionics' Mint supports both, and Tandem supports both, so Dexcom's historical pump-channel exclusivity is gone and its remaining advantages are incumbency and having 15 Day integrated today ([MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/insulets-omnipod-integrate-dexcom-abbott-cgms-automated-smartphone-controlled-dosing); [MedTech Dive, Beta Bionics Mint](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/beta-bionics-insulin-patch-pump-gets-fda-nod/830332/)). No public evidence of Insulet-Dexcom commercial friction was found.

### Regulatory, quality and legal: both files are thick; Dexcom's is older and has stopped growing

Insulet's 2026 file is concentrated and recent: two voluntary corrections (March 12 and May 26) for cannula and tubing tears at the Acton plant causing silent under-delivery, both classified Class I, covering about 7 million pods (8.5% of 2025 production) with 29 and 24 serious injuries reported respectively and no deaths, a $41.0M net charge in H1 2026 against an estimated $60-70M total cost mostly in 2026, and a securities class action filed July 2, 2026 with no accrual ([FDA, Class I database](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfRES/res.cfm?id=220614); [Insulet Q2 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001145197/000114519726000169/podd-20260630.htm)). On the litigation side, the EOFlow trade-secret case went from a $452M jury verdict (December 2024) to a $59.4M award with a worldwide injunction (April 2025) to a precedential Federal Circuit reversal on May 28, 2026 holding the claim time-barred because Insulet "knew or should have known" the facts by 2019; Insulet petitioned for rehearing on July 29, the $59.4M was never booked, and a partial stay keeps the injunction in place for Korea, the EU and the Gulf pending rehearing ([Federal Circuit opinion](https://www.cafc.uscourts.gov/opinions-orders/25-1807.OPINION.5-28-2026_2700697.pdf); [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/divided-federal-circuit-panel-nixes-452-million-trade-secret-judgment)). Dexcom's file is longer and older: the March 4, 2025 FDA warning letter on an uncleared sensor-material change that FDA found "performs worse for accuracy," which remains unresolved in the public record (no close-out letter, re-inspection or 483 found; the FY2025 10-K still says only "received"); three Class I recalls in 2025 (receivers, the G7 iOS app, the G6 Android app) and smaller 2026 actions; the Hunterbrook short reports; two securities class actions (2024 guidance cut, 2024-2025 design changes), consolidated derivative suits and consolidated consumer G7 class actions, all at the pleading stage; and one fully closed item, the December 2024 Abbott patent settlement with royalty-free cross-licenses to 2034 ([FDA warning letter 700835](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/dexcom-inc-700835-03042025); [Dexcom 10-Q Q2 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001093557/000109355726000143/dxcm-20260630.htm); [Dexcom 8-K, Dec 20, 2024](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355724000197/dxcm-20241220.htm)). The relevant difference for investors is direction: Dexcom's quality metrics (complaint rates, NPS up three quarters running, no new recall tied to the coating issue) have been improving for a year and the market has stopped pricing them, while Insulet's corrections are six months old, their retention side-effects are still working through the numbers, and the class action is at its earliest stage.

### Governance and management: both have new CEOs; Insulet's team is newer and has skin in the game

Insulet's leadership is almost entirely new: Ashley McEvoy (ex-J&J MedTech worldwide chairman) became CEO in April 2025, Flavia Pease (ex-Charles River CFO, an Insulet director since 2024) became CFO on September 30, 2025, Elizabeth Weatherman has chaired the board since 2022, Jay Mazelsky (IDEXX executive chair) joined in July 2026 and two long-serving directors left in 2026 ([Insulet CEO release](https://investors.insulet.com/news/news-details/2025/Insulet-Appoints-Ashley-McEvoy-President-and-CEO/default.aspx); [Insulet 8-K, June 24, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001145197/000114519726000148/podd-20260624.htm)). Insiders own about 0.3%, but the only open-market transactions in twelve months are three purchases after drawdowns: director Michael Minogue $500K at $246 (February 25, 2026), director Timothy Stonesifer $400K at $143.51 (June 3, 2026) and McEvoy $162K at $147.47 (August 21, 2026), with no insider sales found in 2026 ([MarketBeat PODD insider trades](https://www.marketbeat.com/stocks/NASDAQ/PODD/insider-trades/); [Motley Fool, CEO purchase](https://www.fool.com/coverage/filings/2026/08/24/insulet-ceo-ashley-mcevoy-buys-usd160-000-in-shares-after-55-stock-decline-what-is-this-telling-investors/)). No activist stake or takeover interest was found. Dexcom's transition is settled: Jake Leach (21-year veteran, ex-CTO and COO) became CEO on January 1, 2026, Kevin Sayer returned as executive chairman in March 2026, CFO Jereme Sylvain added the COO title in September 2026, all twelve directors were re-elected with ~88% say-on-pay support in May, and Elliott Investment Management disclosed a large stake and settled for two board seats on May 14, 2026 with an operational rather than break-up agenda ([Dexcom 8-K, Sept 10, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001093557/000109355726000175/dxcm-20260910.htm); [MedTech Dive, Elliott](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-to-add-2-board-directors-with-activist-investor-elliott/820374/)). Dexcom insiders are net sellers under 10b5-1 plans at rising prices; the one flag is that Elliott's share count and its two directors' identities remain unverified. The capital-allocation contrast is the sharpest governance signal: Dexcom is net cash, has repurchased about $2.35B since October 2023 and has a formal policy of directing at least half of FCF to buybacks, while Insulet's $300M accelerated repurchase in Q1 2026 was executed at about $240 a share, 43% above today's price, roughly $115M remains on the authorization and management "looks at all options" but has declined to commit to a program ([Insulet Q2 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001145197/000114519726000169/podd-20260630.htm); [Investing.com, Wells Fargo conference](https://ng.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/insulet-at-wells-fargo-conference-guidance-cut-meets-longterm-optimism-93CH-2690101)). Insulet could afford more: with consensus FCF of $397M and $494M in FY2026-2027 and a $750M undrawn revolver, it could fund $300-500M a year of repurchases and still be net cash by FY2027, and at 17.7x forward earnings the arithmetic of buying back stock is far better than it was at $240.

## Scenarios: Insulet's $90-260 range against Dexcom's $55-135

The set-up going into the scenarios is that both stocks trade close to their consensus base cases but with opposite revision momentum. ## Relative value: Insulet offers the higher expected return, Dexcom the higher-confidence one

Put side by side, the scenario math says Insulet offers roughly twice Dexcom's probability-weighted return (about +20% against +10%) and a more asymmetric distribution (upside-to-downside ratio at the scenario midpoints of about 3.2:1 against Dexcom's 1.4:1), and its bear case is a smaller percentage loss (central -23%) than Dexcom's (central -28%) even though its bear probability is higher. That last point is the crux of the relative-value case. Dexcom's multiple has already re-rated 20-25% from its trough on the strength of three clean quarters, so its bear case requires only that the 2026 re-rating reverse, which realized history shows can happen on fundamentals no worse than today's; Insulet's multiple has compressed 76% in twelve months to the 0th percentile of its own history and to a discount to diversified medtech, so its bear case requires the market to conclude that a company growing 20%-plus with 19% operating margins deserves a 12-16x multiple, which is a more demanding proposition. Insulet's base case also has a built-in tailwind that Dexcom's lacks: because consensus FY2027 EPS growth is 18.5% and the multiple is 17.7x, a year of simply meeting numbers with an unchanged multiple delivers roughly 18% from earnings alone, whereas Dexcom at 27.8x on 17% EPS growth delivers a similar earnings contribution but with a multiple that is more likely to contract than expand.

The case for Dexcom is that its return is far more certain and its risks are better understood. It has now delivered three consecutive beat-and-raise quarters with a margin recovery that is visible in the numbers rather than promised; it generates $1.4B of FCF (a 4.3% yield) against Insulet's $266M (3.1%, and depressed); it is net cash with a 2.6% buyback yield and a formal capital-return policy against Insulet's net debt and a spent authorization; its competitive picture is improving at the margin (Abbott slowing, share recapture on 15 Day) while Insulet's is deteriorating (three tubeless launches in nine months); its quality problems are a year older and its metrics have turned; and its bull case rests on a discrete, external, well-flagged catalyst (the CMS non-insulin Type 2 decision) rather than on a commercial execution fix whose progress the company has not yet quantified. Its sell-side support is also far stronger (89% Buy, 4.6% short interest) against Insulet's split coverage and 7.4% short interest, which matters for how each stock trades around its Q3 print ([StockAnalysis DXCM forecast](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/dxcm/forecast/); [StockAnalysis PODD forecast](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/podd/forecast/)). And Dexcom carries an option Insulet does not: if Insulet's Type 2 onboarding problem proves structural, the patients who drop a pod mostly keep a sensor, so Dexcom's Type 2 exposure is largely insulated from the outcome, while the reverse is not true.

The conditions under which each is preferred follow from that. Insulet is the better risk/reward for an investor who can hold through two more quarters of negative revision momentum and who reads the Type 2 problem as an onboarding failure (fixable by the 45-day compensation change, insurance navigation and Discover) rather than a structural feature of the patient population; the clearest confirmation would be a Q3 2026 print in late October or early November that holds the 14-16% US guide and the 12-17% exit range without a further cut and begins disclosing retention metrics, after which the multiple has room to move from 18x toward the low 20s on unchanged estimates. It is also preferred if the 2027 competitor launches slip or arrive with weak reimbursement, or if management commits to a new buyback at these prices, either of which would remove a pillar of the bear case. Dexcom is the better choice for an investor who wants a compounding, net-cash, cash-returning business with lower variance and who is prepared to accept that the easy re-rating has happened; it becomes decisively preferred if CMS issues a favorable proposed decision on non-insulin Type 2 coverage before year-end, or if Insulet's Q3 print cuts again, since Insulet's downside in that event is a move toward $105-120 before the base case can re-assert itself. Two structural cautions apply to both. The 2028 competitive-bidding round touches Dexcom's ~15% Medicare fee-for-service revenue directly and Insulet's ~60% Medicare/Medicaid Type 2 cohort indirectly through Part D plan design, and neither company has quantified the impact ([MedTech Dive, final rule](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/cms-final-rule-diabetes-competitive-bidding/806719/)). And the two stocks are correlated on their shared risks (GLP-1 headlines, tariffs, CMS, Abbott's sensor deals), so owning both is less diversification than it looks; the sensible framing is that Insulet is the higher-beta way to own the same AID/CGM adoption curve, priced today at a discount that the last three years of Dexcom history suggest will close only when the company proves, over several quarters, that the growth it reports is growth it keeps.

## Conclusion

The most useful thing this comparison reveals is that the two stocks are the same trade at different points in its life cycle. Dexcom in September 2026 is where Insulet will be if its retention fix works: a company that took a 40-61% hit for losing its 20%-growth multiple, spent a year proving that margins and execution were intact, and was rewarded not with a return to the old multiple but with a 28x that its 11-13% growth can support. Insulet in September 2026 is where Dexcom was in late 2025: seven weeks past the cut, with the multiple already at a trough, the sell side still cutting, short interest at a record, and the first "prove it" quarter ahead. The relative-value answer is that Insulet's expected return is higher because the market has already paid it the compliment of assuming the worst, but the confidence interval around that return is far wider, and the next two prints decide which tail the distribution collapses toward.

The second takeaway is that the valuation inversion is only partly about growth rates converging. Insulet's 36% P/E discount to Dexcom persists with a three-point growth advantage because the market is charging for three things Dexcom has already resolved and Insulet has not: whether its new patient cohort stays, whether its channel stays uncontested, and whether its cash conversion recovers to the 90% of net income that consensus assumes. Each of those has a dated test within the scenario window: the Q3 and Q4 2026 prints for retention, the Tandem, Beta Bionics and MiniMed launches for the channel, and the FY2026 FCF outturn (guided "down modestly" from $377.7M) for conversion. If Insulet passes all three, the discount to Dexcom is unjustified and the base-to-bull range applies; if it fails the first, the discount widens toward the bear case regardless of the other two. What no source could close, and where the next re-rating in either direction will originate, are the undisclosed numbers: Insulet's Type 2 retention rate and active-user count, the Dexcom/Abbott sensor split on Omnipod 5, the status of Dexcom's FDA warning letter, and the CMS coverage memo that management expects before year-end but that has not yet appeared.
