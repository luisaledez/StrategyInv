# Merged Rayonier Trades Below Private Timberland Marks

*Research report as of September 27, 2026. Prices are September 25, 2026 closes unless stated. Figures marked "(estimate)" are the author's arithmetic from cited inputs, not company or consensus numbers; "(unverified)" marks claims that could not be confirmed against a primary source.*

**Rayonier (NYSE: RYN) closed at $18.88 on September 25, 2026, a fresh 52-week low, 58% below its April 2022 all-time closing high of $45.44 and 30% below its 52-week high of $27.06 — and it is now a fundamentally different company from the one that set that high.** On **January 30, 2026, Rayonier completed an all-stock merger of equals with PotlatchDeltic (PCH)**, issuing ~140.9 million shares (1.8185 RYN shares plus $0.61 cash per PCH share), roughly doubling its acreage to ~4.1 million acres, tripling its share count to ~299 million, and adding six sawmills and a plywood mill; PotlatchDeltic no longer trades, and the combined company kept the Rayonier name and RYN ticker ([Rayonier closing release](https://ir.rayonier.com/news/news-details/2026/Rayonier-and-PotlatchDeltic-Announce-Closing-of-Merger-of-Equals/default.aspx)). The decline from 2022 has three roots that outweigh everything else: a 10-year Treasury that went from ~2.9% to ~5.1%, re-pricing a 2.5%-yielding land stock into a 5.5%-yielding one; a structural collapse in Southern pine pulpwood (South-wide stumpage down ~46–50% from the 2022 peak as more than ten pulp mills closed); and a capital-return program that paid $3.40 per share of special dividends roughly 75% in new stock, mechanically lowering the share price while the merger then diluted per-share metrics further. Against Weyerhaeuser (WY), Rayonier now screens cheaper on run-rate EV/EBITDA (~14–16x vs ~18–20x, estimate) and yields more (5.5% vs 4.2%), but carries less lumber torque, weaker sell-side conviction, and a dividend that consumes roughly 85–100% of plausible 2026 cash available for distribution (estimate). The market values the whole enterprise at ~$1,740 per acre before any credit for mills or real estate, versus ~$2,600 per acre in recent Southern institutional transactions and ~$4,000 per acre in Rayonier's own August 2026 Washington sale, implying a 30–40% discount to private marks (estimate) — though private cash yields of 1.55% against 5% Treasuries suggest those marks, not the public price, may be the ones that eventually move. Our base case (12-month) is $20–24 with the dividend held; bear is $12–15 on a dividend reset and 7% required yield; bull is $26–32 on synergy delivery, sustained $500+/MBF lumber and a visible NAV-realization path.

## The January 2026 PotlatchDeltic merger tripled the share count and ended pure-play status

The single most important thing to understand about Rayonier today is that its 2022–2025 financial history describes a company that no longer exists. Rayonier and PotlatchDeltic announced an all-stock merger of equals on **October 14, 2025** at 1.7339 RYN shares per PCH share, an 8.25% premium implying $44.11 per PCH share, a $7.1B pro forma equity value and **$8.2B pro forma enterprise value including $1.1B of net debt**, with ~54% ownership going to legacy Rayonier holders and ~46% to legacy PotlatchDeltic holders ([Rayonier merger announcement](https://ir.rayonier.com/news/news-details/2025/Rayonier-and-PotlatchDeltic-to-Combine-in-an-All-Stock-Merger-of-Equals-to-Create-a-Leading-Land-Resources-REIT/default.aspx)). Because Rayonier simultaneously declared a $1.40 special dividend (25% cash / 75% stock) to distribute the gain on its New Zealand sale, the exchange ratio was adjusted to **1.8185 shares plus $0.61 cash**; stockholders approved on January 27, 2026 and the deal closed January 30, 2026, ahead of the late-Q1/early-Q2 schedule ([Stockholder approvals release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/52827/000005282726000010/rayonierandpotlatchdeltica.htm); [Q1 2026 10-Q via StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/RYN/10-q-rayonier-inc-quarterly-earnings-report-243d28cfd3ed.html)). Rayonier is the accounting acquirer; total consideration was ~$3.28B, with **140,872,342 shares issued** and ~4.45M OP units, valued at $3.25B in stock plus ~$47M cash, or $41.96 per PCH share at closing versus $44.11 at announcement ([Q2 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000052827/000005282726000099/ryn-20260630.htm); [Jacksonville Daily Record](https://www.jaxdailyrecord.com/news/2026/feb/05/rayonier-paying-325-billion-for-potlatchdeltic-in-merger-of-equals/)). After a review of alternatives, the board announced on March 31, 2026 that the company would keep the Rayonier name and RYN ticker ([Rayonier IR](https://ir.rayonier.com/news/news-details/2026/Rayonier-Maintains-Current-Name-and-Ticker-Symbol-Introduces-New-Logo/default.aspx)).

The combined company reports **four segments**: Southern Timber (3.2M acres, 77% of land: Arkansas 938K, Georgia 850K, Alabama 387K, Florida 346K, Texas 275K, Louisiana 175K, Mississippi 131K, South Carolina 66K), Northwest Timber (930K acres: Idaho 623K, Washington 301K, Oregon 6K), Wood Products (six sawmills with ~1.2 BBF of lumber capacity in Arkansas, Idaho, Michigan and Minnesota plus a 150 MMSF plywood mill), and Real Estate (rural HBU sales plus the Wildlight, Heartwood and Chenal Valley master-planned communities) ([Q1 2026 investor presentation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/52827/000005282726000040/rayonier1q26investorpres.htm)). New Zealand was sold on June 30, 2025 and sits in discontinued operations. Governance is split: Mark McHugh (Rayonier CEO since April 2024) leads the company, PotlatchDeltic's Eric Cremers is Executive Chairman for 24 months post-close, PotlatchDeltic's Wayne Wasechek is CFO, the board is five-and-five, and headquarters move to Atlanta in early 2027 ([Closing release](https://ir.rayonier.com/news/news-details/2026/Rayonier-and-PotlatchDeltic-Announce-Closing-of-Merger-of-Equals/default.aspx)). Management targets **$40M of annual run-rate synergies within 24 months**, at least half by year-end 2026, and said on the August 2026 call it is "on track" ([Motley Fool Q1 2026 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/06/01/rayonier-ryn-q1-2026-earnings-transcript/); [Investing.com Q2 2026 transcript summary](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-rayonier-q2-2026-profit-misses-forecast-as-stock-falls-93CH-4843144)).

The first full post-merger quarter shows the new earnings mix. **Q2 2026 Adjusted EBITDA was $123.7M**: Southern Timber $52.6M, Northwest Timber $26.3M, Wood Products $25.0M, Real Estate $38.3M, less $17.7M corporate; H1 2026 Adjusted EBITDA was $217.8M and H1 CAD was $177.1M ([Q2 2026 earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000052827/000005282726000094/exhibit9912q2026.htm)). Timber is now ~60% of segment EBITDA, and Wood Products — a lumber-price-levered manufacturing stream that did not exist in the legacy REIT — is ~15–20%, with Idaho sawlogs additionally indexed to lumber. Management quantified the sensitivity at roughly **$12M of Wood Products EBITDA plus ~$3M of Idaho log EBITDA per $10/MBF change in lumber** ([Investing.com Raymond James conference summary](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/rayonier-at-raymond-james-conference-strategic-growth-and-synergies-93CH-4541680)). Q2 lumber realizations jumped 18% sequentially to $505/MBF and Wood Products posted its best quarter since PotlatchDeltic's Q3 2022 ([Motley Fool Q2 2026 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/13/rayonier-ryn-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). Management has declined to give a full-year EBITDA or CAD number, citing lumber volatility, and instead guides Southern harvest 12.2–12.5M tons, Northwest 2.0–2.2M tons, ~1.1 BBF of lumber shipments (11 months) and **Real Estate Adjusted EBITDA of $180–200M** ([Q2 2026 earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000052827/000005282726000094/exhibit9912q2026.htm)). Stitching H1 actuals to guided Q3 volumes and Q2 unit economics implies **full-year 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of roughly $450–490M (estimate)**; this is the author's arithmetic, not guidance.

The balance sheet is investment grade but closer to its leverage ceiling than the legacy company ever was. At June 30, 2026 Rayonier had $1.86B of debt, $411.8M of cash, ~$1.45B net debt, net debt/EV of 18%, BBB/Baa3 ratings, and no maturities until 2027 after repaying a $200M term loan from cash in April ([Q2 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000052827/000005282726000099/ryn-20260630.htm)). The Q1 2026 presentation cited a ~2.4% weighted-average cost of debt and ~4-year average maturity, but Q2 interest expense of $16.9M annualizes to ~3.3–3.6% on average debt (estimate), and the assumed PotlatchDeltic term loans carry variable 5.2–5.9% coupons before swaps — so the 2.4% figure should be treated as a hedged, net-of-patronage number that could not be reconciled to the 10-Q debt note ([Q1 2026 investor presentation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/52827/000005282726000040/rayonier1q26investorpres.htm)). On run-rate EBITDA of $450–490M, net debt/EBITDA is roughly 3.0–3.2x (estimate) against a stated <3.0x target, which explains why H1 buybacks of **$103.5M (4.94M shares at ~$20.96)** were funded from cash on hand, and why only $126M of authorization remained at June 30 ([Q2 2026 earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000052827/000005282726000094/exhibit9912q2026.htm)).

## Five legs took the stock from $45.87 to $18.88, and rates drove three of them

Rayonier's peak was **$45.44 on a closing basis (April 20, 2022) and $45.87 intraday (April 21, 2022)**; the September 25, 2026 close of $18.88 is a 58.5% price drawdown ([Yahoo Finance RYN history](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/RYN/history/?period1=1649030400&period2=1651881600&frequency=1d)). That headline overstates the value destruction: roughly $3.40 per share of special dividends ($0.20 in January 2024, $1.80 in January 2025, $1.40 in December 2025) were paid mostly in new shares, and on a dividend-adjusted basis the April 2022 high is $33.96, so the adjusted decline is ~44% (estimate) ([CompaniesMarketCap](https://companiesmarketcap.com/rayonier/stock-price-history/)). Rayonier's own 10-K performance graph puts a five-year total return at exactly **0% (December 2020 to December 2025) versus +96% for the S&P 500, +5% for the FTSE NAREIT All Equity index and -12% for the S&P Global Timber & Forestry index**; from the 2021 peak year to 2025, RYN lost ~30% total return while the NAREIT and Timber indices each lost ~23%, so roughly three-quarters of the underperformance versus the S&P is a sector de-rating, with ~6–7 points of company-specific lag ([Rayonier FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/52827/000005282726000036/ryn-20251231.htm)).

The decline came in five legs. **Leg 1 (April–September 2022, $45.87 to $29.51, -36%)** was the Fed's first hiking cycle and the lumber crash; Rayonier's FY2022 release cited "inflationary pressures across the globe and higher interest rates," New Zealand EBITDA fell 31% on Chinese lockdowns, and long-term debt rose to $1.51B after a $454M, 137,800-acre acquisition ([Q4 2022 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/52827/000005282723000025/exhibit9914q2022.htm)). **Leg 2 (January–October 2023, $36.39 to $24.84, -32%)** coincided with the 10-year Treasury's 5.12% peak; Pacific Northwest EBITDA collapsed 56% to $27.9M as the segment deferred ~150,000 tons of harvest and Southern pulpwood fell 16% on "decreased competition from pulp mills" ([Q4 2023 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/52827/000005282724000006/exhibit9914q2023.htm)). **Leg 3 (March–December 2024, $35.29 to $26.10, -26%)** combined a guidance cut to $275–290M from $290–325M, a 45x-EBITDA sale of 200,000 Washington and Oklahoma acres for $495M, Hurricane Helene's late-September strike on 8.9M acres of Georgia forestland, and a $1.80 special dividend paid ~75% in stock; December 2024 alone was -18% ([Q3 2024 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/52827/000005282724000198/exhibit9913q2024.htm); [BusinessWire, Nov 6, 2024](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241106833816/en/Rayonier-Announces-Timberland-Dispositions-Totaling-495-Million)). **Leg 4 (March–October 2025, $28.31 to $21.89, -23%)** opened with a Q1 2025 miss (Southern Timber EBITDA $27M vs $45M, management citing Helene salvage and mill cutbacks), continued through the $710M New Zealand exit, and ended with the merger announcement, which the market read as negative for RYN: the stock fell 5% on announcement day and slid from $25.44 pre-agreement to $22.74 at closing ([Investing.com Q1 2025 transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-rayonier-misses-q1-2025-earnings-forecast-stock-drops-93CH-4017393); [Jacksonville Daily Record](https://www.jaxdailyrecord.com/news/2026/feb/05/rayonier-paying-325-billion-for-potlatchdeltic-in-merger-of-equals/)). **Leg 5 (January–September 2026, $24.82 to $18.88, -24%)** happened without an earnings collapse — Adjusted EBITDA rose with the merger — but with a dividend reset to $0.26 from $0.2725, the chairman's $3.6M share sale in February, BMO's May target cut citing the "absence of clear near-term catalysts to drive a recovery in southern timber pricing," a 1.2-cent Q2 EPS miss, and finally the September 2026 bond rout ([Investing.com BMO note](https://ca.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/rayonier-stock-price-target-lowered-to-25-by-bmo-on-flat-timber-outlook-93CH-4648859); [MarketBeat insider trades](https://www.marketbeat.com/stocks/NYSE/RYN/insider-trades/)).

The rate story is the common thread. Rayonier's three worst monthly closes since 2022 — September 2022, October 2023 and September 2026 — each fell in a month when the 10-year yield hit a cycle high. On **September 16, 2026 the FOMC raised the funds rate 25bp to 3.75–4.00%**, its first hike since 2023, with the dot plot showing 4.00–4.25% through 2027; the 10-year approached 5.1%, a 19-year high, in the week of September 21–25, and the 30-year mortgage crossed 7.03% on September 24, the first reading above 7% in 20 months ([Federal Reserve statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm); [Washington Trust Week in Review](https://www.watrust.com/articles/week-in-review-september-25-2026); [Freddie Mac PMMS](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/24/3368592/0/en/mortgage-rates-average-7-03.html)). RYN fell 4.0% on September 24 and WY fell 13.8% in September as a whole, so the latest damage is sector-wide. The arithmetic of the re-pricing is stark: at the $45.44 peak the $1.14 regular dividend yielded 2.5%; at $18.88 the $1.04 dividend yields 5.5%, a ~300bp yield expansion that matches the rise in the 10-year from ~2.9% to ~5.1% almost exactly (estimate). Timberland is marketed as an inflation hedge, but in 2026 the discount-rate effect has dominated for the listed equity.

The second root cause is Southern pulpwood, and it is structural rather than cyclical. TimberMart-South's 2Q 2026 South-wide stumpage shows **pine sawtimber at $23.34/ton (~10% below the 2022 peak, below $24 for three straight quarters), chip-n-saw at $16.84 (-20% vs peak) and pine pulpwood at $5.40/ton (-46% vs peak)**, with delivered pulpwood below $30 for four consecutive quarters ([TimberMart-South 2Q 2026 Bulletin](https://timbermart-south.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2Q2026_bulletin.pdf)). The demand side is what changed: more than ten major pulp facilities closed between 2023 and 2025, removing 25M+ tons per year of fiber demand, and Georgia-Pacific's Cedar Springs and International Paper's Savannah and Riceboro closures alone eliminated the market for ~8.3M tons annually, half of it Georgia-sourced — exactly where Rayonier's legacy Atlantic-coast lands sit ([Southern Ag Today, Jan 26, 2026](https://southernagtoday.org/2026/01/26/pine-sawtimber-prices-soften-as-pine-pulpwood-continues-to-fall/); [UGA CAES 2026 Timber Outlook](https://fieldreport.caes.uga.edu/publications/AP130-4-13/2026-timber-forecast/)). Domtar idled its Coosa Pines, Alabama fluff pulp mill in May 2026, adding to the list. Rayonier's own realized Southern weighted-average stumpage fell from $25.74/ton in Q4 2022 to $17.57 in Q4 2025 (-32%), far worse than the South-wide sawtimber decline because Rayonier's mix was pulpwood- and Georgia/Florida-heavy, and its pulpwood realization dropped from $20.95 to $11.70 over the same period ([Q4 2022 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/52827/000005282723000025/exhibit9914q2022.htm); [Q4 2025 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/52827/000005282726000026/exhibit9914q2025.htm)). Sawtimber has not been rescued by 2026's lumber rally because Southern sawmill capacity grew 31% since 2017 while utilization fell from 81% (2Q 2021) to 68% (3Q 2025); Forisk's structural view is that Southern sawtimber inventory sits at ~20 years of removals and the inventory-to-demand ratio "will not dip below 20 over the next ten years" ([Forisk sawtimber supply forecasts](https://forisk.com/sawtimber_supply_forecasts/)). Standalone Rayonier's Adjusted EBITDA fell from $329.8M (2021) to $248.0M (2025, ex-New Zealand); Southern Timber EBITDA dropped 17% and Pacific Northwest EBITDA 63% between 2022 and 2025, and Real Estate (+75%) was the only segment that grew.

The third root cause is self-inflicted in form if not in substance. Management's November 2023 plan to sell $1B of assets to exploit the public/private value gap finished at ~$1.45B, with U.S. timberland sold at a weighted-average 44x three-year Timber EBITDA ([Rayonier 2025 Annual Report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/52827/000005282726000059/rayonier_2025xarxlr.pdf)). That was good capital allocation in principle, but REIT rules forced distribution of the taxable gains, and the two large specials ($1.80 and $1.40) were paid at most 25% in cash, issuing 15,071,426 shares in total and creating the two sharpest monthly drops of the whole decline (December 2024 -18%, October 2025 -17%) ([Q4 2025 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/52827/000005282726000026/exhibit9914q2025.htm)). The headline 11–12% "yields" quoted in 2025–26 news were inflated by those specials; income buyers who arrived for them were then met with two cuts to the regular rate, from $0.285 to $0.2725 (2025) to $0.26 (2026), a cumulative 8.8% reduction ([StockAnalysis dividend history](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ryn/dividend/)). The merger compounded the per-share dilution and removed the "pure-play timber REIT" scarcity value Rayonier had emphasized in 2023, adding a taxable lumber business whose Q2 2026 rebound leaned on Canadian duties and mill curtailments. Sentiment has followed: **short interest rose from 3.45M shares (September 30, 2025) to 13.7M (September 15, 2026), roughly doubling as a share of the enlarged float to 4.6%**, insiders have only sold ($4.1M over 12 months, no buys) despite management's repeated "significant discount to NAV" language, and the sell-side has drifted to Hold with targets clustered at $23–25 ([Equibles short interest](https://equibles.com/stocks/ryn/short-interest); [GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9096424/rayonier-inc-ryn-stock-down-40-now-undervalued-gf-score-66100)).

On valuation, Rayonier is modestly cheaper than Weyerhaeuser on every anchor that can be computed cleanly. Gross EV per acre is ~$1,740 versus ~$1,860 (estimate), and both figures include non-timber businesses, materially so for Weyerhaeuser, whose Wood Products segment earned $129M in Q2 2026 and $250M in FY2025 against Rayonier's $25M quarter ([WY Q4 2025 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/106535/000119312526029351/wy-ex99_1.htm)). On run-rate EV/EBITDA Rayonier sits at ~14–16x versus Weyerhaeuser at ~18–20x (estimate), and it yields 130bp more. The headline trailing multiples on data aggregators (20.9x for RYN, 28.1x for WY) are unreliable for both: RYN's trailing EBITDA lacks seven months of PotlatchDeltic, and WY's aggregator EBITDA ($688M) is well below its company-reported Adjusted EBITDA (~$950M–1.05B trailing, estimate). Rayonier's GAAP payout ratio of 231–301% is an accounting artifact of depletion on stepped-up timber basis — H1 2026 DD&A was $126.7M versus $46.9M a year earlier — and the relevant coverage metric is CAD, discussed below ([Q2 2026 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000052827/000005282726000099/ryn-20260630.htm)). Weyerhaeuser is the more levered balance sheet at 5.1x net debt/Adjusted EBITDA (trough-cycle), yet carries a one-notch-higher Moody's rating and the stronger sell-side backing: 75% of covering analysts rate WY Buy versus 33% for RYN, with ~43–55% implied upside to WY's consensus versus ~30–38% for RYN ([StockAnalysis WY forecast](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/wy/forecast/); [StockAnalysis RYN forecast](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ryn/forecast/)). The Street is evidently waiting for Rayonier to show a clean post-merger run-rate and synergy delivery before re-rating it.

Structurally, the merger moved Rayonier toward Weyerhaeuser's model without getting all the way there. Wood Products was ~20% of Rayonier's Q2 2026 segment EBITDA versus ~42% at Weyerhaeuser, and Weyerhaeuser sells more lumber in one quarter (1,081 MMBF in Q1 2026) than Rayonier ships in a year ([Investing.com WY Q1 2026 slides](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/weyerhaeuser-q1-2026-slides-ebitda-surges-on-climate-deal-93CH-4674831)). Each $10/MBF of lumber is worth ~$15M to Rayonier (including Idaho indexed logs) versus ~$50M to Weyerhaeuser, so Weyerhaeuser remains the higher-beta lumber play and Rayonier's mills function more as a hedge that internalizes Idaho and Arkansas log demand ([GuruFocus via TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/gurufocus:74f2b2573094b:0-weyerhaeuser-why-the-timber-giant-s-50m-per-10-lumber-sensitivity-makes-it-a-value-trap/)). CEO McHugh has said the company intends to keep, not divest, the mills, calling them "another lever to optimize our overall portfolio value over time" ([Nareit](https://www.reit.com/news/articles/rayonier-sees-multiple-ways-to-maximize-portfolio-value-post-merger)). The trade-off cuts the other way on timber geography: Rayonier's 77% Southern weighting puts it squarely in the weakest stumpage backdrop, while Weyerhaeuser's 2.5M Western acres retain Douglas-fir and Japanese export exposure that Rayonier largely gave up through its 2023–24 Oregon/Washington sales and the 2025 New Zealand exit (Rayonier now holds 6K acres in Oregon and, after the August 2026 exchange, roughly 265K in Washington). Weyerhaeuser also has a more mature "natural climate solutions" franchise, with Climate Solutions Adjusted EBITDA of $119M in 2025 and a $250M target by 2030, whereas Rayonier's land-based solutions remain, in management's words, "in the nascent stages" ([WY Q4 2025 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/106535/000119312526029351/wy-ex99_1.htm)).

PotlatchDeltic's record explains why its holders accepted the deal and why they may now regret the timing. PCH was the best performer of the three timber REITs from end-2021 (-16.5% to its delisting versus -37.5% for RYN and -41.6% for WY on price, estimate), because its lumber-levered, Idaho-indexed model paid off in 2021 (a $4.00 special dividend on record lumber) and held up better in 2025, and because it received an 8.25% takeover premium ([CompaniesMarketCap PCH](https://companiesmarketcap.com/potlatchdeltic/stock-price-history/); [PCH 8-K Dec 2021](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1338749/000095017021005037/pch-ex99_1.htm)). A PCH holder who kept the 1.8185 RYN shares plus $0.61 cash now holds ~$35.55 of value against a $41.73 last PCH print and a $51.35 all-time high, and the largest such holder to act — Executive Chairman Cremers — sold 169,200 shares at $21.44 on February 27, 2026. Among the manufacturers, West Fraser's negative trailing EBITDA (-$240M) and LPX's -20% share price show that pure producers had a harder 2025–26 than either REIT's timber segment, which supports the REIT model's lower volatility but also its lower upside if lumber runs.

## Three anchors: $1,740 per acre, ~15x forward EBITDA, and a dividend that CAD barely covers

The NAV-per-acre anchor is where the bull case lives, and it is also the anchor with the widest error bars. Private-market evidence from 2025–26 clusters tightly in the U.S. South: Fastmarkets RISI puts institutional Southern transaction prices at ~$2,600 per acre in 2025, up ~50% from ~$1,750 in 2018; Weyerhaeuser sold 86,000 Georgia/Alabama acres at ~$2,558 per acre and bought 117,000 North Carolina/Virginia acres at ~$3,111; and Rayonier itself bought 57,000 Alabama/Texas acres from Resource Management Service in August 2026 for $146M, or ~$2,561 per acre, while selling 36,000 Washington acres for $145M, or ~$4,028 per acre ([MetLife Investment Management](https://investments.metlife.com/insights/agricultural-finance/whats-supporting-timberland-values/); [WY Oct 30, 2025 release](https://investor.weyerhaeuser.com/2025-10-30-Weyerhaeuser-Provides-Update-on-Timberlands-Portfolio-Optimization-Actions); [Rayonier IR, Aug 5, 2026](https://ir.rayonier.com/news/news-details/2026/Rayonier-Completes-Transactions-to-Optimize-Timberland-Portfolio/default.aspx)). Pacific Northwest marks are higher and more dispersed: Weyerhaeuser sold Oregon acreage at ~$3,931 (June 2026) and ~$6,786 (October 2025) per acre and bought mature Washington land at ~$9,500, while Rayonier's 2023 Oregon sale fetched ~$4,400 ([WY Q2 2026 release](https://investor.weyerhaeuser.com/2026-07-30-Weyerhaeuser-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results)). No recent public mark exists for Idaho, where 623K of Rayonier's 930K Northwest acres sit and where values are generally below coastal PNW and lumber-linked.

Against these marks, the market values all of Rayonier at **~$1,740 per acre gross (EV $7.13B / 4.1M acres, estimate)**. Netting even a conservative $0.4–0.6B for Wood Products (6–7x a $60–90M mid-cycle EBITDA, estimate) and $0.3–0.5B for the three master-planned communities pushes the implied timberland value toward **~$1,500 per acre (estimate)**, roughly 40% below the RISI Southern average and below what Rayonier just paid in Texas and Alabama. Two cross-checks temper the mid and high columns. BMO's $25 target is explicitly set at a 15% discount to its NAV, implying a BMO NAV near $29.40 per share (estimate), and an unverified mid-2026 secondary summary put the stock at a "27% discount to estimated NAV" when it traded near $21, implying ~$29 as well ([Investing.com BMO note](https://ca.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/rayonier-stock-price-target-lowered-to-25-by-bmo-on-flat-timber-outlook-93CH-4648859); [Seeking Alpha listing, unverified](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4908985-rayonier-a-land-rich-reit-trading-below-its-asset-value)). Sell-side NAVs therefore appear to cluster near the low end of the private-mark range, presumably applying a higher discount rate than appraisers do. That is the crux of the NAV debate: Forisk's September 2026 analysis shows private timberland's EBITDDA cash yield at 1.65% in 2025 and 1.55% trailing at Q2 2026 against Treasuries "recently topping 5%," the widest gap in the index's 38-year history, while transaction volumes have fallen to ~1M acres per year from a 2.8M-acre long-term average and Forisk notes buyers are "finding it increasingly difficult to underwrite acquisitions at these levels" ([Forisk, Sept 17, 2026](https://forisk.com/timberlands-cash-yield-vs-u-s-treasuries-a-38-year-gut-check/); [Forisk 2025 Timberland Transactions Review](https://forisk.com/2025-timberland-transactions-review/)). A public discount of 30–50% to appraisal-type values is real, but part of it is the public market pricing a repricing that private appraisals have not yet recorded. Rayonier's management has nonetheless monetized the gap repeatedly — selling at 44–45x EBITDA, buying back stock at ~$21 — and the CFO said in August 2026 that the stock trades "at a significant discount to net asset value."

The EV/EBITDA anchor is cleaner but requires a run-rate. On the author's $450–495M 2026 pro forma Adjusted EBITDA band, Rayonier trades at **~14.4–15.8x forward EV/EBITDA (estimate)**. That is above the 9–12x five-year band cited for Weyerhaeuser, but the two are not comparable: Weyerhaeuser's multiple is dragged down by its large manufacturing business, whereas pure timberland transactions historically clear at 25–50x EBITDA (management's own framework cites a 6–7% total real return built from a 2–4% EBITDA yield, 4–5% biological growth and ~2% real price appreciation) ([Q1 2026 investor presentation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/52827/000005282726000040/rayonier1q26investorpres.htm)). A blended fair multiple for a company that is ~60% timber, ~15–20% lumber and ~25–30% lumpy real estate is plausibly 13–17x in a 5% rate world, which brackets the current price — the market is not obviously mispricing the earnings stream; it is discounting the asset base.

The dividend/CAD anchor is where the bear case lives. The $0.26 quarterly dividend on ~299M shares commits **~$311–316M per year**, and management has framed it as a "sustainable dividend that grows over time" while citing 33 consecutive years of payments ([Rayonier Q1 2026 dividend release](https://ir.rayonier.com/news/news-details/2026/Rayonier-Announces-First-Quarter-2026-Dividend/default.aspx)). H1 2026 CAD was $177.1M with only five months of PotlatchDeltic; a full-year figure of roughly $320–380M (estimate) is plausible given a full second half and guided Q4 real estate closings, which puts the **CAD payout ratio at roughly 82–98% (estimate)** and the CAD yield on the $5.65B market cap at 5.7–6.7% (estimate). Coverage is adequate in the base case and thin in any downside: standalone Rayonier's 2025 CAD was $198.6M, so a reversion of Real Estate toward its 2024–25 standalone run-rate ($92–127M) combined with lumber back at Q1 2026's $427/MBF would drive CAD below the dividend. Buyback capacity ($126M remaining, ~2% of market cap) is likewise dependent on asset-sale proceeds rather than free cash flow. Consensus 2026/2027 Adjusted EBITDA and CAD could not be obtained (paywalled); only FY2026 EPS of $0.42 and revenue of $1.52B were visible ([StockAnalysis forecast](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ryn/forecast/)).

Land-based solutions are an NAV "plus" rather than an earnings anchor. The combined company has **~77,000 acres under solar option (down from 80,000 at Q1 as weaker options lapse), of which only ~600 acres are actually leased, and ~154,000 acres under CCS lease**; ~35,000 acres of solar options mature through 2028, with the "first significant step up" in 2027 ([Motley Fool Q2 2026 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/13/rayonier-ryn-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). Realized value so far arrives as one-off land sales to solar developers at ~$10,100 per acre (2,200 acres in Q1 2026, 459 acres in Q2) inside the Real Estate segment, not as recurring rent; management frames solar at ~10x and CCS at ~5x core timber value per acre but discloses no dollar pipeline, calls data centers "incremental more so than transformative," and Weyerhaeuser's analogous CCS project is not expected to inject before ~2029 ([Motley Fool Q1 2026 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/06/01/rayonier-ryn-q1-2026-earnings-transcript/)). Illustratively, if 25–50% of the solar-option acreage converts at $700–1,000 per acre per year, that is $14–40M of high-margin rent (estimate), or $200–400M of land value if sold at ~$10,000 per acre — meaningful to NAV, immaterial to 2026–27 EBITDA. Legacy PotlatchDeltic's February 2025 lithium/bromine lease on ~900 Arkansas Smackover acres (5,000–7,000 acres prospective) is a further option with no disclosed economics ([PotlatchDeltic lithium lease release](https://ir.rayonier.com/news/news-details/2025/PotlatchDeltic-Executes-LithiumBromine-Lease-Agreement-in-Arkansas/default.aspx)).

The bear case is a dividend story before it is an earnings story. Its logic is that the combination of a $311M fixed distribution, ~$1.45B of net debt approaching the 3.0x ceiling, a Real Estate segment that management admits is Q4-concentrated and lumpy, and a lumber business whose Q2 rebound relied on curtailments and duties leaves no cushion if two of those variables turn together. Real Estate is the swing factor: standalone Rayonier's Real Estate EBITDA was $72.7M in 2022 and $92.4M in 2024, so the $180–200M guide is well above any historical base, and rural HBU pricing already softened from $8,340 to $6,290 per acre year over year in Q2 2026. A pulpwood slide toward $3–4/ton would also cut land expectation values 13–38% by University of Georgia estimates, eroding the NAV floor the bull case relies on ([Wood Central / Journal of Forestry](https://woodcentral.com.au/pine-pulpwood-stumpage-gap/)). A negotiated Canada–U.S. lumber settlement is the specific policy risk: lumber remained a "sticking point" in late-September 2026 trade talks, and Weyerhaeuser's CEO has said restarted Canadian volume is not expected — which is exactly the consensus a deal would upend ([Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/canada/article-new-tariffs-trade-trump-leblanc-negotiations-washington/)). **Bear signposts**: Q3 2026 results (due ~November 4) showing Wood Products EBITDA below ~$15M or Real Estate below the $25–35M guide with the full-year range cut; Q4 2026 CAD below ~$300M or a 2027 dividend declaration below $0.26; net debt/EBITDA guided above 3.0x; a U.S.–Canada softwood agreement; two or more additional Southern pulp mill closures; the 10-year sustained above 5.25%; and short interest continuing to climb past 5% of float.

The base case is simply that the current run-rate holds, the dividend is maintained, and the stock earns its yield plus a modest re-rating as post-merger comparables become clean in 2027. The evidence favors it: harvest guidance was narrowed rather than cut through 2026, Real Estate has beaten its quarterly guide twice, synergies are "on track," management is buying back stock, and the balance sheet has no maturities until 2027 with the next towers in 2028 ($300M) and 2029 ($390M) ([Q1 2026 investor presentation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/52827/000005282726000040/rayonier1q26investorpres.htm)). The base case does not require Southern timber to recover — only to stop deteriorating — and it does not require rates to fall, only to stop rising; at a 5.0–5.5% dividend yield the stock is worth $19–21, and a 30–35% discount to a ~$30–33 NAV gives $20–23. **Base signposts**: Q3 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $110–125M with lumber realizations at $500+/MBF; reaffirmed $180–200M Real Estate guide; a Q1 2027 dividend declaration at $0.26 or higher; disclosed synergies of $20M+ run-rate by year-end 2026; continued buybacks at $18–21; and 2026 full-year CAD of $330M or more.

The bull case rests on NAV realization rather than earnings, and it now has a structural tailwind it lacked a year ago: with PotlatchDeltic absorbed, there are only two listed U.S. timber REITs, TIMOs bought 55% of acres transacted in 2025 while selling 15%, and Rayonier's own management has repeatedly demonstrated that private buyers pay 44–45x EBITDA for what the public market prices at ~15x ([Wood Central](https://woodcentral.com.au/timberland-investors-us-housing-recovery-2026/)). The bull path runs through three channels: continued tax-efficient exchanges and asset sales at $2,500–4,000 per acre that shrink the share count (each $150M of sales at private marks buys back ~2.5% of the company at $19); the 2027 solar-option maturities converting into disclosed lease income or $10,000-per-acre sales; and, less likely but not negligible, an unsolicited take-private by a pension or TIMO consortium at a 15–25% discount to a $30–36 NAV, which would still be a $23–30 price. Operationally, the bull case needs lumber to hold above $520/MBF with Canadian duties intact (each $10/MBF adds ~$15M), Southern sawlog demand to shift to U.S. mills as Weyerhaeuser and Rayonier both expect, and the Fed to pivot in 2027. **Bull signposts**: another large timberland disposition or exchange at $2,500+ per acre in the South or $4,000+ in the Northwest; a buyback authorization increase above $200M; a disclosed multi-thousand-acre solar lease or data-center site sale; Wood Products EBITDA of $35M+ in a quarter; Southern pine sawtimber stumpage moving above $25/ton on TimberMart-South; Raymond James' Strong Buy joined by a second Buy-rated firm; and any 13D filing or strategic-review disclosure.

## Conclusion

Rayonier at $18.88 is not a broken business but a re-rated one, and the re-rating has two layers that investors should separate. The first is a sector-wide discount-rate shock that has taken every long-duration land asset down with it — Weyerhaeuser sits at its own 52-week low — and that will reverse only when the 10-year Treasury stops making 19-year highs. The second is Rayonier-specific and largely self-authored: a decade of pure-play positioning was traded for scale, lumber exposure and a tripled share count at the exact moment Southern pulpwood was undergoing a structural demand collapse, and the capital returned along the way arrived mostly as new shares that the market treated as dilution rather than distribution. The merger's logic — buy back stock at ~15x EBITDA with proceeds from land sold at 45x, spread corporate costs across 4.1M acres, and add mills that internalize log demand — is coherent, but it will not be believed until the combined company posts four clean quarters and shows the $40M of synergies in the corporate line.

What has changed in the analysis is that the NAV argument is now necessary but not sufficient. The 30–50% discount to private marks is real and management is acting on it, yet private timberland's 1.55% cash yield against 5% Treasuries means the public market may be leading rather than lagging, and a bull thesis that depends on appraisal values holding must also explain why transaction volumes have fallen to a third of normal. The more durable edge is optionality the market assigns near-zero value to: 77,000 solar-option acres reaching their first maturity wave in 2027, 154,000 CCS acres, a two-name public sector attracting private capital, and a management team with a demonstrated willingness to sell assets into the gap. The dividend is the fulcrum — held, the stock compounds at 5.5% while waiting for those options to mature; cut, it re-prices to the bear range regardless of what timberland is "worth." The Q3 report in early November and the Q1 2027 dividend declaration are therefore the two data points that matter most between now and year-end.
