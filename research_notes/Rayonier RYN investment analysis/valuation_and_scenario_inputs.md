## Key Question 3: Southern sawtimber and pulpwood outlook 2026-2028; what flips the oversupply

### Takeaway
South-wide pine sawtimber stumpage is ~$23/ton (flat to slightly down, ~10% below the 2022 peak) and expected "relatively stable" through 2026; pine pulpwood stumpage has collapsed to ~$5.40-6/ton (-46% vs 2022) and is expected to keep drifting lower in most areas. Forisk's structural view is that Southern sawtimber inventory sits at ~20 years of removals (5 years above pre-recession) and the inventory/demand ratio stays above 20 for a decade; the flip requires sustained net sawmill capacity growth (+2.1 BBF 2026-28) plus housing recovery, while pulpwood needs new bioenergy/pellet or mill demand to offset ~9.6M tons of closed pulp/paper capacity.

## Key Question 4: Canadian softwood lumber duties in 2026 and the impact on US lumber production and log demand

### Takeaway

### Inferences
- With PCH's Idaho logs indexed to lumber, roughly a quarter of RYN's timber EBITDA is now lumber-price-linked on top of the Wood Products segment itself, raising earnings volatility versus the pre-merger profile; the report writer should model Northwest timber and Wood Products jointly against lumber scenarios.
- Buyback capacity ($126M) is ~2% of market cap; with CAD near the dividend, further buybacks likely depend on asset-sale proceeds (land exchanges, HBU) rather than free cash flow.
- No activist presence plus a balanced legacy board reduces near-term governance catalysts but also means a take-private would need to be management/board-led or unsolicited.
