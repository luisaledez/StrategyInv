# Dexcom (DXCM) stock decline and partial recovery, Sept 2023 - Sept 2026: event-by-event timeline

Scope note: this file covers the chronology of material stock-moving events, what management said, how analysts reacted, and the multiple-compression question. Price moves quoted from the assignment (e.g. "-40.7% on 2024-07-26") are the coordinator's own close-to-close data; where press sources quote a different figure (typically after-hours or intraday), both are given. Research was done 2026-09-27.

Quick reference (all figures sourced in the sections below):
- FY2023 revenue $3.62B -> FY2024 $4.03B (+11%) -> FY2025 $4.66B (+16%) -> TTM (June 2026) $4.97B; 2026 guide $5.18-5.25B.
- Trailing P/E now ~34x, forward ~31x (Sept 2026), vs a forward P/E of ~26x at the Nov 2025 low and a P/E of ~41x in Feb 2026 per press. TTM EPS now $2.54.
- Every leg down after April 2024 was a guidance/margin/quality event, not a revenue collapse: revenue never fell year-over-year in any quarter of the period (the worst was +2% in Q3 2024).

## Pre-period context (2021 peak to Sept 2023) and 2024-04-26 (-9.9%): Q1 2024 results

### Takeaway
Q1 2024 was a beat-and-raise (revenue +24% to $921M, low end of guidance lifted), but the stock had run to ~$138-140 and expectations were higher still; the after-hours drop (~7% to $128.40) was widely described as an "expectations" selloff rather than a fundamental problem. Nothing in the print foreshadowed July.

### Cited Findings
- Q1 2024 revenue $921.0M, +24% reported; U.S. and international both +24% reported (international +26% organic); GAAP operating income $140.2M (15.2% of revenue) vs $78.6M a year earlier — [Dexcom Q1 2024 press release](https://investors.dexcom.com/news/news-details/2024/Dexcom-Reports-First-Quarter-2024-Financial-Results/default.aspx); [8-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355724000078/dxcmq1202499-1_3312024.htm)
- FY2024 revenue guidance raised at the low end: from $4.15B floor to $4.20-4.35B (17-21% growth); gross margin, operating margin and adjusted EBITDA margin guidance reiterated — [Dexcom Q1 2024 press release](https://investors.dexcom.com/news/news-details/2024/Dexcom-Reports-First-Quarter-2024-Financial-Results/default.aspx)
- Adjusted EPS $0.32 beat by 5 cents; revenue beat the $909.2M forecast; net income $146.4M more than tripled y/y; shares fell ~7% to $128.40 after hours on April 25, 2024 — [Drug Delivery Business](https://www.drugdeliverybusiness.com/dexcom-stock-slides-beats-q1-2024/)
- BTIG called the after-market reaction "too harsh," saying the market had set "a relatively high bar" and citing growth drivers ahead (core insulin-intensive adoption, Stelo debut) — [Drug Delivery Business](https://www.drugdeliverybusiness.com/dexcom-stock-slides-beats-q1-2024/)
- Japan was flagged by management as an expected key growth driver in coming quarters — [Yahoo Finance Q1 2024 call transcript](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/dexcom-dxcm-q1-2024-earnings-053012694.html)

### Inferences
- The -9.9% close-to-close move on April 26, 2024 (coordinator figure) exceeded the ~7% after-hours drop reported by the trade press; the incremental selling appears to be valuation-driven (stock near its 2024 high of ~$140, trailing P/E near 90x per the assignment) rather than tied to any disclosed negative.
- In hindsight, Q1 2024 was the last "clean" quarter before the sales-force realignment (which Sayer later said began earlier in 2024) hit new-patient starts.

### Gaps
- I did not find a press source quantifying the full-day April 26, 2024 move or attributing it to a specific line item (e.g. margin or Japan); trade coverage only discusses the ~7% after-hours drop.
- Detailed 2021-2023 context (2021 peak ~$163, 2022 drawdown) was out of scope and not researched.

## 2024-07-26 (-40.7%): Q2 2024 miss and guidance cut

### Takeaway
Dexcom missed Q2 revenue (~$1.00B vs ~$1.04B consensus), cut FY2024 revenue guidance from $4.20-4.35B to $4.00-4.05B (organic growth 17-21% -> 11-13%), and attributed it to three self-inflicted, U.S.-centric issues: a sales-force realignment that disrupted physician relationships, faster-than-expected G7 rebate eligibility that lowered revenue per patient, and share loss in the DME channel; ~70,000 fewer new patient starts than planned. The stock fell ~41%, its worst day since the 2005 IPO, wiping ~$18B of market value, and drew multiple downgrades.

### Cited Findings
- Q2 2024 revenue ~$1.0B (+15% y/y) missed the ~$1.04B target; FY2024 guidance cut from $4.20-4.35B to $4.00-4.05B; shares fell >40% on July 26, 2024, the worst single day since the 2005 IPO, erasing ~$18B of market cap — [CNBC, July 26, 2024](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/07/26/dexcom-shares-plunge-more-than-40percent-after-q2-earnings.html); [CNBC, July 25, 2024](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/07/25/dexcom-shares-plummet-30percent-after-company-lowers-fiscal-year-guidance.html)
- Three stated causes: (1) sales-force restructuring to focus on high-prescribing physicians vs. primary care, which caused "significant disruption"; (2) lower revenue per customer from increased rebates; (3) DME channel share loss "particularly affecting high-revenue patient segments." Company reported ~70,000 fewer patient starts than expected and U.S. revenue ~$40M below internal estimates; revised organic growth 11-13% vs prior 17-21%; Q2 net income $143.5M — [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-q2-earnings-sales-forecast-shortfall/722540/)
- CEO Kevin Sayer: "We have higher expectations for our business than what we experienced this quarter." CFO Jereme Sylvain called the patient-acquisition shortfall "a pretty sizable" miss — [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-q2-earnings-sales-forecast-shortfall/722540/)
- Sayer said the sales-force reshuffle changed geographic coverage so physicians were dealing with different reps and the company was "short a large number of new patients"; management also cited faster-than-expected uptake of G7 rebate programs lowering near-term revenue per patient — [CNBC, July 26, 2024](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/07/26/dexcom-shares-plunge-more-than-40percent-after-q2-earnings.html)
- Rebate eligibility for G7 moved "three times quicker than for the G6 model"; international sales also fell short partly due to stronger type 1 penetration; Sayer raised and then dismissed the GLP-1 explanation, pointing to internal problems instead — [Motley Fool, July 30, 2024](https://www.fool.com/investing/2024/07/30/dexcom-stock-plunged-eli-lilly-novo-nordisk/)
- J.P. Morgan's Robbie Marcus on the call: "I'm just kind of in shock at how big of a disruption and a downward guide it is," later characterizing the issues as "self-inflicted" rather than market-driven — [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-q2-earnings-sales-forecast-shortfall/722540/)
- Reported downgrades/target cuts on July 26, 2024 (per aggregated analyst-rating feeds surfaced in search; not verified against the primary notes): Baird to Neutral from Outperform, PT $161 -> $80; J.P. Morgan to Neutral from Overweight, PT $145 -> $75; Piper Sandler kept Overweight, PT $150 -> $90; Morgan Stanley Equal-Weight, PT $120 -> $75 — [Benzinga DXCM analyst ratings](https://www.benzinga.com/quote/DXCM/analyst-ratings); downgrades also reported by [Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4129136-dexcom-stock-falls-downgrades-guidance-cut) (paywalled; not fetched)
- Fierce Biotech framed it as "CGM sales challenges" — [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/dexcom-stock-drops-40-after-reporting-cgm-sales-challenges)

### Inferences
- The size of the move relative to a ~6% guidance cut reflects (a) a very high starting multiple, (b) loss of credibility (guidance had just been raised in April), and (c) the fact that the three causes were execution-related and therefore raised doubts about management rather than the market.
- Analysts treated the "self-inflicted" framing as double-edged: recoverable, but a sign of weak visibility into new-patient dynamics and channel mix (DME vs pharmacy).

### Gaps
- Exact Q2 2024 consensus revenue figure varies by source (~$1.04B); I did not fetch the call transcript for verbatim quotes beyond those reported by CNBC/MedTech Dive/Motley Fool.

## Aug-Dec 2024: aftershocks (Abbott-Medtronic deal, Q3 2024, class actions, Abbott settlement)

### Takeaway
The second half of 2024 added a competitive shock (Abbott-Medtronic exclusive CGM/pump partnership, DXCM -8% on Aug 7), a weak Q3 (+2% revenue, U.S. -2%), and securities class actions, but ended with a positive: a royalty-free global patent settlement with Abbott.

### Cited Findings
- Aug 7, 2024: Abbott announced a global partnership to integrate Libre-based sensors exclusively with Medtronic insulin delivery systems — [Abbott press release](https://abbott.mediaroom.com/2024-08-07-Abbott-Enters-Global-Partnership-to-Connect-Its-World-Leading-Continuous-Glucose-Monitoring-System-with-Medtronics-Insulin-Delivery-Devices); Dexcom finished that day down ~8%; analysts saw limited harm since Dexcom cannot convert existing Medtronic pump users but its installed base was not seen at material risk — [MD+DI](https://www.mddionline.com/diabetes/what-abbott-medtronic-s-collaboration-means-for-diabetes-tech); [Drug Delivery Business](https://www.drugdeliverybusiness.com/abbott-medtronic-partner-to-connect-cgm-insulin-delivery-systems/)
- Stelo, the first OTC CGM for non-insulin type 2/prediabetes, launched Aug 2024 — [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-abbott-otc-cgms-diabetes-tech/726715/)
- Q3 2024 (reported Oct 24, 2024): revenue $994.2M, +2% reported / +3% organic; U.S. revenue -2%, international +12% reported / +16% organic; adjusted EPS $0.45 vs $0.43 consensus; GAAP operating margin 15.3% (down 580 bp); FY24 guidance held at $4.00-4.05B; Sayer: results "in line with our expectations as our team responded quickly to the business dynamics that emerged earlier this year"; Investing.com reported a 16% after-hours drop (see gap) — [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/dexcom-q3-earnings-beat-estimates-but-stock-tumbles-16-on-weak-growth-3681745); [8-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355724000179/dxcmq3202499-1_9302024.htm)
- Q3 2024 call: DME channel "experienced share loss, although trends stabilized late in the quarter" — [Yahoo/GuruFocus call highlights](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/dexcom-inc-dxcm-q3-2024-072100298.html)
- Securities class actions: three complaints filed Aug 21 - Oct 9, 2024 in S.D. Cal., consolidated Dec 13, 2024 (In re Dexcom, Inc. Class Action Securities Litigation, No. 24-cv-1485); consolidated complaint (Jan 27, 2025) alleged false statements from April 28, 2023 to July 25, 2024 about FY2024 revenue expectations; motion to dismiss granted with leave to amend May 14, 2025; derivative suits filed Sept 2024 - April 2025 — [Dexcom 10-Q Q3 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355725000280/dxcm-20250930.htm); [Lieff Cabraser case page](https://www.lieffcabraser.com/securities/dexcom/)
- Dec 20, 2024: Dexcom and Abbott signed a settlement and patent cross-license resolving all outstanding patent litigation worldwide; worldwide, royalty-free, non-exclusive, fully paid-up licenses; no royalties or other payments; mutual covenant not to sue until Dec 20, 2034 — [Dexcom 8-K, Dec 20, 2024](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355724000197/dxcm-20241220.htm); [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/abbott-dexcom-agree-10-year-truce-over-diabetes-sensor-patent-litigation); [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/abbott-dexcom-settle-cgm-patent-lawsuits/736300/)

### Inferences
- The Abbott settlement removed a tail risk (injunction/royalty exposure) but also removed a bull-case option (Dexcom collecting royalties from Abbott), consistent with it being a non-event for the multiple.

### Gaps
- The "16% after-hours" figure for Q3 2024 in the Investing.com headline is not corroborated by CNBC ("shares fall"); the coordinator's close-to-close data does not list Oct 25, 2024 as a >9% day, so the actual full-day move was likely much smaller. Treat the 16% as unverified.

## Jan-Feb 2025: preliminary Q4 2024, 2025 guide, Q4 2024 margin miss

### Takeaway
2025 guidance ($4.60B, +14%; gross margin 64-65%) was in line, but Q4 2024 non-GAAP gross margin collapsed to 59.4% (from 64.2%) on damaged-in-transit inventory and low-yield build configurations, the first of a string of margin/quality surprises that dominated 2025.

### Cited Findings
- Preliminary Q4 2024 (Jan 13, 2025): revenue at least $1.113B (+8%); U.S. ~$803M (+4%); international ~$310M (+17%); FY2024 ~$4.032B (+11%); Q4 gross margin hit by a non-cash charge "primarily related to inventory damaged in transit and certain build configurations that lowered production yield"; 2025 outlook: revenue $4.60B (~+14%), non-GAAP GM ~64-65%, non-GAAP OM ~21% — [Dexcom 8-K exhibit, Jan 13, 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355725000004/dxcm991_1132025.htm); [Dexcom press release](https://investors.dexcom.com/news/news-details/2025/Dexcom-Reports-Preliminary-Unaudited-Results-for-the-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2024-and-Initial-2025-Outlook/default.aspx)
- Q4 2024 final (Feb 13, 2025): revenue $1.114B (+8%); GAAP gross margin 58.9% vs 63.5%; non-GAAP gross margin 59.4% vs 64.2% — [Dexcom 8-K exhibit, Feb 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355725000029/dxcm12312024-exhibit991.htm)
- Morgan Stanley set an $82 target on Feb 14, 2025 — [Simply Wall St](https://simplywall.st/stocks/us/healthcare/nasdaq-dxcm/dexcom/future)

### Inferences
- 2024 was, per Investing.com's SWOT summary, the slowest growth year in a decade (+11%), so the 2025 guide of +14% was read as a modest re-acceleration but still far below the 20%+ the multiple had been built on.

### Gaps
- No press source found quantifying the stock reaction to the Feb 13, 2025 print (not a >9% day in the coordinator's data).

## 2025-03-10 (-9.1%): FDA warning letter

### Takeaway
An FDA warning letter dated March 4, 2025 (8-K filed Friday March 7; stock fell 9.1% on Monday March 10) cited manufacturing/quality-system non-conformities at San Diego and Mesa, AZ, most notably an unauthorized change to the sensor's resistance-layer coating that failed its own equivalence study's primary endpoint. It did not restrict production or sales, but it seeded the "quality" narrative that Hunterbrook exploited six months later.

### Cited Findings
- Warning letter dated March 4, 2025; 8-K filed March 7, 2025; follows FDA inspections of Mesa, AZ (June 10-14, 2024) and San Diego (Oct 21 - Nov 7, 2024) and "deficiencies in the response letters" to the Form 483; letter cites "non-conformities in manufacturing processes and quality management system"; company said no material impact expected to manufacturing capacity or 2025 revenue guidance; letter "does not restrict the Company's ability to produce, market, manufacture or distribute products," does not require recalls, but "additional legal or regulatory action may be taken without further notice" — [Dexcom 8-K, March 7, 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355725000047/dxcm-20250304.htm)
- FDA said Dexcom changed a component used in the resistance layer of its sensors and, in a clinical study meant to show equivalence, "did not meet its primary endpoint"; stock fell ~7% in after-market trading to ~$73.50 — [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/dexcom-receives-fda-warning-letter-over-cgm-manufacturing-issues)
- Letter posted publicly March 26, 2025: FDA said "sensors with the changed coating cause higher risks for users who rely on the sensors to dose insulin"; Dexcom had internally manufactured the coating compound for supply-chain redundancy but failed to adequately validate it; Dexcom stopped distributing G7 with the new component and reverted to the external supplier, but FDA said its response did not address affected G6 sensors; other findings covered monitoring procedures for glucose/acetaminophen testing and prior G6 dissolved-oxygen deficiencies. J.P. Morgan's Marcus said the issues "could be addressed quickly" and might cause "minor delays" to the 15-day G7 clearance — [MedTech Dive, March 2025](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-warning-letter-cgm-coating-change/743597/)
- Dexcom publicly rejected the claim that the changes were unauthorized — [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-rejects-claims-fda-warning-letter/743769/)
- Stock continued to decline in the days after the letter — [Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4419048-dexcom-continues-decline-following-fda-warning-letter) (paywalled)

### Inferences
- The market initially treated the letter as a contained regulatory issue (~7-9%), consistent with the company's "no restriction" language; its real cost showed up later through elevated scrap/quality spending (Q2-Q3 2025 gross margins) and as the factual backbone of the September 2025 short report and the 2025 class action.

### Gaps
- Status of the warning letter (whether FDA has closed it out as of Sept 2026) was not found in the sources I reached.

## 2025-04-03 (-9.3%) and 2025-04-09 (+10.1%): tariff shock and tariff pause

### Takeaway
Both moves were macro: the April 2-3 "reciprocal tariff" announcement did not exempt medical devices and diabetes-device makers (Dexcom, Insulet, Tandem) "bore the brunt"; on April 9 the 90-day pause produced the Nasdaq's second-best day ever (+12.2%). The G7 15-Day FDA clearance was announced the next day (April 10), so it was not the cause of the April 9 gain.

### Cited Findings
- Despite lobbying for a carve-out, medical devices were swept into the tariffs; "diabetes device makers are bearing the brunt," naming Dexcom, Insulet and Tandem, while large cardiac/ortho names saw little movement; analysts anticipated near-term margin pressure — [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/medical-device-industry-swept-up-tariffs-diabetes-device-makers-bear-brunt)
- April 9, 2025: Trump announced a 90-day pause and 10% reciprocal rate for most countries (China to 125%); S&P 500 +9.52% (biggest since 2008), Nasdaq +12.16% (second-best day ever), Dow +7.87% — [Nasdaq.com](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/us-stocks-spike-trump-announces-90-day-tariff-pause-some-countries); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/04/09/nx-s1-5357405/stocks-tariffs-china-eu-trump-trade-war)
- April 10, 2025: FDA cleared Dexcom G7 15 Day (15.5-day wear, 8.0% MARD), launch expected 2H 2025 — [Dexcom press release](https://investors.dexcom.com/news/news-details/2025/Dexcom-G7-15-Day-Receives-FDA-Clearance-the-Longest-Lasting-Wearable-and-Most-Accurate-CGM-System/default.aspx); [Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/fda-clears-dexcom-g7-cgm-15-day-wear-2025a10008mx)
- Q1 2025 commentary (May 1): "higher shipping costs and some tariff impact around raw materials" pressured margins — [Drug Delivery Business](https://www.drugdeliverybusiness.com/dexcom-stock-mixed-q1-2025-repurchase/)
- The stock's 2025 YTD low was April 29, 2025, from which it rallied >27% into the summer — [Barchart via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/dexcom-stock-turnaround-showing-real-173502610.html)

### Inferences
- Dexcom's above-market beta on both days reflects (a) its overseas manufacturing footprint (Malaysia/Ireland per the Morningstar framing of diabetes-device exposure) and (b) a compressed, sentiment-driven shareholder base after the 2024 crash.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain a Dexcom-specific quantification of tariff cost exposure (e.g. a $-figure or bp of gross margin) from management for 2025.

## 2025-05-02 (+16.2%): Q1 2025 results and $750M buyback

### Takeaway
Q1 2025 delivered a revenue beat ($1.036B, +14% organic; U.S. +15%), record new-patient starts led by non-insulin type 2, expanded PBM coverage, a $750M buyback and reaffirmed revenue guidance, despite a 1-cent EPS miss and a cut in gross-margin guidance to ~62%. Press coverage recorded only a ~3% after-hours gain, so most of the +16% came during the May 2 session as the "back to its old self" narrative took hold off a depressed base.

### Cited Findings
- Q1 2025 revenue $1.036B, +12% reported / +14% organic; U.S. +15% ($750.5M), international +7% reported / +12% organic; board authorized a $750M repurchase (through June 30, 2026) — [Dexcom press release, May 1, 2025](https://investors.dexcom.com/news/news-details/2025/Dexcom-Reports-First-Quarter-2025-Financial-Results-and-Announces-750-Million-Share-Repurchase-Program/); [8-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355725000111/dxcm03312025-exhibit991.htm)
- Adjusted EPS $0.32 vs $0.33 consensus; FY revenue guide $4.6B maintained; non-GAAP gross margin guide cut to ~62% (from 64-65%) on supply-chain normalization costs; after-hours +3% to $72.30, then -1.6% pre-market; BTIG: "Dexcom is getting back to its old self"; drivers: record new customer additions, two of three largest PBMs now covering CGM for any diabetic, Stelo Amazon launch, U.S. revenue well ahead of consensus — [Drug Delivery Business](https://www.drugdeliverybusiness.com/dexcom-stock-mixed-q1-2025-repurchase/)
- Record new-customer starts, particularly in the type 2 non-insulin population; stock +3.2% after hours to $72.51 — [Investing.com call summary](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-dexcom-q1-2025-revenue-beats-expectations-93CH-4018426)
- Zacks: "DexCom stock rises despite Q1 earnings miss & lower margins" — [Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/2461538/dexcom-stock-rises-despite-q1-earnings-miss-lower-margins) (bot-blocked; headline only)

### Inferences
- The +16% day is best explained as a relief rally: the stock had made its YTD low on April 29 (three days earlier), short interest was elevated after the warning letter, and the print showed the July-2024 problems (new starts, U.S. growth) had been fixed. The margin cut was forgiven because it was framed as transitory.

### Gaps

## May-June 2025: receiver recall (Class I)

### Takeaway
A speaker defect in G6/G7/ONE/ONE+ receivers (missed hypo/hyper alarms) led to an Urgent Medical Device Correction on May 12, 2025 and FDA Class I designation on June 16, 2025, covering >700,000 receivers; it cost ~100 bp of Q2 2025 gross margin and fed the quality narrative.

### Cited Findings
- Dexcom issued an Urgent Medical Device Correction May 12, 2025; FDA classified it Class I on June 16, 2025; affected receivers may fail to sound alerts for dangerous glucose levels due to defective foam or an assembly error causing the speaker to lose contact with the circuit board; >700,000 recalled receivers distributed worldwide; at least 56 reported injuries; potential consequences include seizures, loss of consciousness and death — [FDA recall notice](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/medical-device-recalls-and-early-alerts/continuous-glucose-monitor-receiver-recall-dexcom-inc-removes-certain-dexcom-g6-g7-one-and-one); [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-recall-cgm-receivers-lack-alarm/751051/)
- Q2 2025 gross margin included a receiver recall charge of ~100 bp — [Nasdaq/Zacks](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/dexcom-stock-falls-despite-q2-earnings-beat-2025-sales-view-raise)

### Inferences
- Because most G7 users rely on the phone app rather than a receiver, the revenue impact was small; the significance was reputational and in gross margin.

### Gaps
- None material.

## 2025-07-31 (-9.3%): Q2 2025 results, gross margin 60.1%, CEO succession announced

## Overall: multiple compression vs fundamentals, and the structural bear concerns

### Takeaway
The three-year round trip is almost entirely multiple compression: revenue rose from ~$3.6B (FY2023) to ~$5.0B TTM and TTM EPS is now $2.54, while the trailing P/E fell from ~90x at the April 2024 peak (per the assignment) to ~34x today. The bear case rests on (1) Abbott's larger and growing Libre share plus its exclusive Medtronic pump tie-up, (2) mix-driven revenue-per-patient erosion as growth shifts to type 2 basal/non-insulin and OTC (Stelo), (3) GLP-1 demand fears, (4) a two-year run of quality/manufacturing problems (warning letter, Class I recall, scrap), and (5) Medicare competitive bidding from 2028.

### Inferences
- Decomposition (using the assignment's ~$140 peak at ~90x trailing and StockAnalysis' current $86.62 at 34.15x on $2.54 TTM EPS): implied TTM EPS at the peak was ~$1.55; EPS has since grown ~64% while the multiple fell ~62%, so price fell ~38%. In other words, fundamentals contributed roughly +64% and the multiple roughly -62%; the entire decline and more is multiple compression. On revenue the same holds: TTM revenue +~40-47% ($3.4-3.6B -> $5.0B) while P/S fell from double digits to ~6.6x.
- Why the multiple compressed: growth downshifted from 20%+ (pre-2024 expectation) to 11-16% at the same time that (a) execution credibility was damaged (July 2024 guide cut, two 2025 GM guide cuts, "below the Street" 2026), (b) the quality file thickened (warning letter, Class I recall, short reports, class action), and (c) two structural overhangs appeared (Abbott share momentum incl. Medtronic exclusivity; Medicare competitive bidding 2028). The rebound since Q2 2026 reflects margin recovery and record starts, not a return to 20% growth, which is why the multiple has only partially recovered (~31x forward vs ~26x at the low).
- Stelo cannibalization: no source quantified it; given $100M first-year Stelo revenue vs $4.66B total and the fact that Stelo targets non-insulin users who mostly lacked coverage, cannibalization appears immaterial so far (inference).
- Activist involvement: none surfaced in any source; the governance changes (COO/CFO combination, new director Boehnlein) appear to be internal succession moves.
