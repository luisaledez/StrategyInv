# Dexcom (DXCM): Regulatory, Quality, Manufacturing, Legal and Governance Issues, Jan 2023 – Sep 27, 2026

Research cutoff: September 27, 2026. Facts are drawn from FDA databases, SEC filings (8-K/10-K/10-Q/DEF 14A), company press releases, trade press (MedTech Dive, Fierce, MassDevice/Drug Delivery Business, Law360/Bloomberg Law) and, where flagged, from a short-seller (Hunterbrook) and plaintiff law firms. Allegations are labeled as such.

## 1. FDA Warning Letter (March 2025): findings, commitments, status

### Takeaway
FDA issued a warning letter dated March 4, 2025 after 2024 inspections of Mesa, AZ (June 10-14, 2024) and San Diego (Oct 21 - Nov 7, 2024), citing seven QSR (21 CFR 820) violations plus a premarket-notification (807.81) violation for an uncleared change to the sensor resistance-layer material that FDA said made G6/G7 sensors less accurate. Dexcom disputed the "design change" characterization, said it stopped shipping G7 sensors made with the second-source material, and has submitted multiple responses. As of the latest filings (10-K FY2025 filed Feb 2026; 10-Q for Q2 2026), the letter is still described only as "received in March 2025" with no disclosure of close-out, and I found no public FDA close-out letter or any 2025-2026 follow-up inspection/Form 483 in the sources reviewed.

### Cited Findings
- Warning letter (FDA ref. 700835) dated March 4, 2025; inspections at San Diego, CA (Oct 21 - Nov 7, 2024) and Mesa, AZ (June 10-14, 2024). Numbered violations: (1) 820.75(b) failure to monitor glucose and acetaminophen concentrations during functional acceptance testing of G6/G7 sensors; (2) 820.75(a) test-method validation recorded pass/fail rather than measured values, measurement uncertainty not incorporated into slope acceptance criteria; (3) 820.30(c) design inputs failed to define special controls of 21 CFR 862.1355(b); G7 MARD specs relied on G6 pivotal data despite a different algorithm; (4) 820.30(i) design change: Dexcom replaced one material with another in the sensors' resistance layer; the initial clinical study did not meet primary endpoints; additional in-vivo studies (TDR-1000254) showed the new material "performs worse for accuracy"; standard deviation of glucose sensitivity was "twofold" that of the original material; Dexcom ceased G7 commercial distribution with the new material "but did not address G6 sensors"; (5) 820.30(d) inadequate coating-thickness specs (criteria applied to average thickness across sensors, not individual sensors); (6) 820.30(g) hazard analyses (RA-000258 Rev. 033, RA-000548 Rev. 41) did not address automated insulin dosing (AID) use; (7) 820.100(a) CAPA-000426 (opened April 19, 2024) on G6 dissolved-oxygen content out of specification; FDA rejected Dexcom's conclusion that residual risk from hypoxic compression events was acceptable. Premarket violation (807.81(a)(3)): the material change was a major modification requiring a new 510(k) (original clearances K182041 for G6, K213919 for G7 used only the original material). FDA required a written response within 15 business days, continuing CAPA updates, a new premarket submission for the material change on both G6 and G7 ("not promised by Dexcom" per FDA), and said a follow-up inspection would verify corrections. — [FDA warning letter, Dexcom Inc. 700835, 03/04/2025](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/dexcom-inc-700835-03042025)
- Dexcom's 8-K (March 4, 2025): the letter "cited deficiencies in the response letters" Dexcom sent after the Form 483s; the company "takes the matters identified in the warning letter seriously," had "already submitted multiple responses," was preparing a written response, and would "continue to undertake certain corrections and corrective actions" with regular FDA updates. The letter does "not restrict the Company's ability to produce, market, manufacture or distribute products, require recall of any products, nor restrict the Company's ability to seek FDA 510(k) clearance"; Dexcom did "not expect a material impact" on manufacturing capacity or 2025 revenue guidance, while cautioning it cannot guarantee FDA will accept its response or give a resolution timeline. — [Dexcom 8-K, March 4, 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355725000047/dxcm-20250304.htm)
- Dexcom said it "received FDA observations related to process improvement and documentation requests" and had implemented process controls in response to the 483s. — [MedTech Dive, Mar 2025](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-fda-warning-letter/741993/)
- Warning letter was publicly posted by FDA on March 26, 2025; per J.P. Morgan's Robbie Marcus, the component is "a chemical compound that the sensor wire is dipped in"; Dexcom had begun producing it internally for supply redundancy and reverted to the external supplier. FDA also said sensors with the new component "cause higher risks for users who rely on the sensors to dose insulin." — [MedTech Dive, Mar 26, 2025](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-warning-letter-cgm-coating-change/743597/)
- Dexcom's rebuttal: "no design changes were made"; "Dexcom qualified a second source for one of its raw materials, developed since 2021, to ensure an uninterrupted supply to customers," and "extensive testing" showed the material met specifications; the company said it had disclosed modifications through informal and formal 510(k) submissions under non-significant-change guidance, and did not expect the letter to impede the pending 15-day G7 review. — [MedTech Dive, Mar 2025 (rebuttal)](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-rejects-claims-fda-warning-letter/743769/)
- FY2025 10-K (filed Feb 2026) still describes the matter only as: "In March 2025, we received an FDA warning letter following inspections of our facilities in San Diego, California, and Mesa, Arizona relating to observed non-conformities in manufacturing processes and our quality management system," with no disclosure of close-out, re-inspection or new 483s. — [Dexcom 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355726000027/dxcm-20251231.htm)
- The Q2 2026 10-Q (period ended June 30, 2026) contains no discussion of the warning letter, Form 483s, inspections or recalls. — [Dexcom 10-Q Q2 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001093557/000109355726000143/dxcm-20260630.htm)
- On the Q3 2025 call (Oct 30, 2025), management did not mention the warning letter; interim CEO Leach said "the quality of the sensors coming off our lines today is exceptional and meets our high standards." On the Q2 2026 call (July 30, 2026) there was no warning-letter or recall discussion; management cited "excellent product performance" and "continued manufacturing efficiencies and quality management." — [Q3 2025 call transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/04/08/dexcom-dxcm-q3-2025-earnings-call-transcript/); [Q2 2026 call transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/03/dexcom-dxcm-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)

### Inferences
- The absence of any close-out disclosure through the Q2 2026 10-Q, combined with FDA's stated intent to re-inspect, implies the warning letter most likely remained open as of mid-2026; however, the 10-Q's silence could also reflect management judging it immaterial. Treat "still open" as likely but unconfirmed.
- Dexcom's public framing ("second-source raw material, not a design change") is directly contradicted by FDA's finding that the change is a major modification requiring a 510(k); FDA also noted Dexcom had not committed to filing that submission. Whether Dexcom ultimately filed one is not disclosed in sources found.

### Gaps
- No FDA close-out letter, re-inspection report, EIR, or 2025-2026 Form 483 for Dexcom was found in searches; FDA's warning-letter page does not indicate closure. The redacted letter ((b)(4)) hides the material names and the change date; Hunterbrook (a short seller) asserts the switch occurred in December 2023 (see Section 4).
- Whether Dexcom submitted a new 510(k) for the resistance-layer material on G6 and G7, and whether affected G6 inventory was withdrawn, is not disclosed.

## 2. Recalls and field actions 2023-2026 (units, injuries, deaths)

### Takeaway
Dexcom had three FDA Class I actions in 2025 (receiver speaker, G7 iOS/ONE+ app "Sensor Failed" alert, G6 Android app crash), one Class II app recall in 2026 (G7 watchOS/ONE iOS alert delay), and a June 2026 correction for two stolen scrap lots of G7 sensors. The receiver recall is the largest (602,445 G7 receivers; ~703,687 receivers across G6/G7/ONE/ONE+) with at least 56 injuries and zero deaths reported by FDA. I found no FDA recall tied specifically to the resistance-layer accuracy issue, and no Class I recall in 2023-2024 in the sources reviewed.

### Cited Findings
- Receiver speaker recall (Class I): Dexcom initiated May 12, 2025; FDA posted June 16, 2025; recall no. Z-1944-2025; 602,445 G7 receivers worldwide (US plus 40+ countries). Cause: "Defective foam or an assembly error may cause the receiver speaker to lose contact with the printed circuit board, leading to missed audible alerts." Remedy: serial-number check and free replacement; status open. — [FDA recall database, G7 receiver](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfres/res.cfm?id=213508); companion entries for [G6 receiver](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfres/res.cfm?id=213506) and [ONE receiver](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfres/res.cfm?id=213871)
- FDA safety communication (posted July 17, 2025): Dexcom sent urgent notification June 9, 2025; affected G6, G7, ONE and ONE+ receivers (20 UDI/lot combinations); "at least 56" injuries, zero deaths reported; classified as most serious recall type. — [FDA recall notice](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/medical-device-recalls-and-early-alerts/continuous-glucose-monitor-receiver-recall-dexcom-inc-removes-certain-dexcom-g6-g7-one-and-one)
- MedTech Dive reported 703,687 units potentially affected across all receiver models. — [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-recall-cgm-receivers-lack-alarm/751051/). (Note: Drugwatch cites "approximately 640,000" receivers; the FDA G7-only figure is 602,445. Numbers differ by scope.) — [Drugwatch, Jan 21, 2026](https://www.drugwatch.com/news/2026/01/21/fda-flags-serious-software-issue-in-dexcom-glucose-monitoring-app/)
- G7 iOS app / ONE+ app "Sensor Failed" alert (Class I): initiated July 24, 2025; posted Sept 4, 2025; recall no. Z-2447-2025; 907,842 units worldwide; the app "ends the CGM sensor session, stops reporting glucose values" without a Sensor Failed alert when the transmitter has a hardware/firmware failure; mandatory app update by Aug 20, 2025. — [FDA recall database](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfres/res.cfm?id=215006)
- G6/G6 Pro Android app v1.15.0 (Class I): initiated Aug 28, 2025; posted Oct 30, 2025; recall no. Z-0318-2026; 333 units (less than 1% rollout); app "can cause the app to terminate unexpectedly" so users may not receive glucose values/alerts; mandatory upgrade by Oct 6, 2025. — [FDA recall database](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfres/res.cfm?id=216085). Drugwatch reports FDA flagged the G6 Android app issue publicly on Dec 29, 2025 as a Class I software correction. — [Drugwatch](https://www.drugwatch.com/news/2026/01/21/fda-flags-serious-software-issue-in-dexcom-glucose-monitoring-app/)
- G7 watchOS app v2.9.0-2.11.2 and ONE iOS app v1.5.0/1.6.0 (Class II): initiated April 14, 2026; posted May 11, 2026; recall no. Z-2164-2026; 58,582 units; "a low-priority file I/O check blocks the higher-priority thread responsible for processing estimated glucose values," delaying alerts; update required by April 30, 2026. — [FDA recall database](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfRes/res.cfm?ID=219289)
- Stolen scrap sensors (June 2026): on May 26, 2026 Dexcom initiated an urgent correction for two G7 sensor lots (1725204004: possibly not properly sterilized, infection risk; 1725069002: may not produce readings) that were designated as scrap, stolen during destruction and resold by an unauthorized distributor (Pharmsource, LLC); users told to stop use and request replacements. — [UPMC Health Plan notice, June 2026](https://www.upmchealthplan.com/providers/medical/announcements-updates/announcements/2026-announcements/2026-06-voluntary-nationwide-recall-dexcom-g7-sensors-june-2026); [Express Scripts notice](https://www.express-scripts.com/notices/recall/dexcom-recalls-stolen-sensors)
- Management on G7 quality (Oct 30-31, 2025): Leach acknowledged out-of-box deployment failures in early 2025 ("When we saw it, we jumped on it and resolved it"), plus Bluetooth and adhesive fixes; "our complaint rates for G7 have been largely stable over the past couple of years"; deployment complaint increases were "offset by decreases in accuracy complaints, Bluetooth complaints." CFO Sylvain said there had "likely been a bit of an impact" on Q3 new patient starts; scrap rates were elevated but improving due to third-party component quality issues. — [MedTech Dive, Oct 31, 2025](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-execs-fixed-g7-quality-problems/804383/); [Q3 2025 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/04/08/dexcom-dxcm-q3-2025-earnings-call-transcript/)
- Q2 2026 call: "G7 net promoter scores increasing in each of the last 3 quarters"; complaint levels declined; 400 bp gross-margin improvement attributed partly to "quality management." — [Q2 2026 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/03/dexcom-dxcm-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)

### Inferences
- The 2025 pattern (warning letter + three Class I actions in one year) is what plaintiff firms and Hunterbrook have built on; management's narrative is that the issues were discrete (foam/assembly, app bugs, early-2025 deployment failures) and resolved.

### Gaps
- No Class I recall directly tied to sensor accuracy (the resistance-layer issue) was found; FDA addressed it via the warning letter rather than a recall. No 2023 or 2024 Dexcom recalls of note surfaced in this research; older FDA entries (e.g., a Class II G6 three-pack entry) were not verified for date.
- Injury/death counts for the app recalls were not given in FDA database entries reviewed.

## 3. G7 15 Day: clearance, launch, delay and accuracy

### Takeaway
FDA cleared G7 15 Day on April 10, 2025 (MARD 8.0%, 15.5 days wear incl. grace period, adults 18+), about five weeks after the warning letter; Dexcom guided to a 2H 2025 launch, piloted it in Q3-Q4 2025 and launched broadly in the US on December 1, 2025. Management said the warning letter would not impede clearance, and the clearance timing supports that; the launch slipped to the back end of 2H 2025 mainly for payer/DME contracting, not a stated regulatory hold. Health Canada clearance came in 2026.

### Cited Findings
- April 10, 2025: FDA cleared Dexcom G7 15 Day for adults 18+, "up to 15.5 days of wear" including a 12-hour grace period, overall MARD 8.0%. — [Dexcom press release](https://investors.dexcom.com/news/news-details/2025/Dexcom-G7-15-Day-Receives-FDA-Clearance-the-Longest-Lasting-Wearable-and-Most-Accurate-CGM-System/default.aspx); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250410304660/en)
- Dexcom told MedTech Dive in March 2025 it did not expect the warning letter to impede the pending 15-day G7 review and anticipated a 2H 2025 launch. — [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-warning-letter-cgm-coating-change/743597/)
- Q3 2025 call (Oct 30, 2025): "we now have contracts finalized with Medicare, every major commercial payer and our commercial DME partners"; a "Warrior community" pilot was under way with broader rollout "in the coming weeks." — [Q3 2025 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/04/08/dexcom-dxcm-q3-2025-earnings-call-transcript/)
- US launch December 1, 2025. — [Diabetech](https://www.diabetech.info/p/the-15-day-dexcom-g7-arrives-in-the-u-s-on-december-1st)
- Q2 2026 (July 30, 2026): rollout "tracking well"; target "nearly 50% of our U.S. customer base to the G7 15 day system by year end"; "sequential uptick in new customer starts in The US"; Health Canada clearance received (first international approval); "great feedback on the new algorithm updated patch, extended wear time." — [Q2 2026 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/03/dexcom-dxcm-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)

### Inferences
- The eight-month gap between clearance (Apr 2025) and launch (Dec 2025) was longer than typical; management attributed it to contracting/pilot sequencing and 2025 quality clean-up, and did not publicly attribute it to the warning letter.

### Gaps
- No specific, sourced tally of 15-day-sensor accuracy complaints was found (Trustpilot/anecdotal reviews only). Whether the 15-day sensor uses the original or the second-source resistance-layer material is not disclosed.

## 4. Short-seller / critical research (Hunterbrook) and MAUDE trends; Dexcom's rebuttal

### Takeaway
Hunterbrook Media published "Dexcom's Fatal Flaws" on Sept 18, 2025 (with Hunterbrook Capital disclosed short) alleging a December 2023 unauthorized coating switch, inferior accuracy and a spike in MAUDE accuracy complaints, followed by an Oct 28, 2025 piece citing three more MAUDE-reported deaths (13 G7 deaths total in MAUDE since 2023). DXCM fell 11.8% on Sept 19, 2025. Dexcom did not respond to Hunterbrook directly; its public rebuttal came via earnings calls (complaint rates "largely stable," accuracy complaints down). A Dexcom-funded clinician (Forlenza) published a peer-reviewed letter criticizing the report's methodology. All Hunterbrook claims beyond what is in the FDA letter are allegations.

### Cited Findings
- Hunterbrook, Sept 18, 2025 (allegations): Dexcom switched the G7 sensor coating from an outsourced to an in-house formulation in December 2023 without FDA clearance; internal testing showed the new membrane coating was worse by "every accuracy metric"; devices made after the change drew 22% more accuracy complaints than expected on market share (Abbott 68% fewer); 13% of G7 complaints concern accuracy vs 6% for G6; "60+ hospitalizations and multiple deaths" per a Morgan & Morgan investigation; Hunterbrook Capital holds a short position in DXCM. Hunterbrook quotes incoming CEO Leach as saying "Things happen" and a VP calling issues "minor things." Dexcom declined comment. — [Hunterbrook, "Dexcom's Fatal Flaws"](https://hntrbrk.com/dexcom/)
- MAUDE analysis method: adverse-event reports from first complaint date through end-2024, NLP used to isolate inaccurate-reading complaints. — [Forlenza letter, Diabetes Technology & Therapeutics](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/15209156251390814)
- Hunterbrook follow-up, Oct 28, 2025: three deaths in MAUDE reports filed Sept 2025 (events May 18, May 30, Aug 30, 2025); "at least 13 G7 deaths" logged in MAUDE since 2023 launch; Hunterbrook petitioned FDA; Dexcom "did not respond to repeated requests for comment." — [Hunterbrook follow-up](https://hntrbrk.com/dexcom2/)
- Stock reaction: DXCM fell $8.99 (11.8%) to $67.45 on Sept 19, 2025. — [Labaton Keller Sucharow](https://www.labaton.com/news-insights/labaton-keller-sucharow-llp-announces-expanded-securities-class-action-lawsuit-filed-against-dexcom-inc-and-certain-executives); Oppenheimer downgraded on Sept 8, 2025 citing accuracy concerns (per Hunterbrook). — [Hunterbrook](https://hntrbrk.com/dexcom/)
- Rebuttal literature: Gregory P. Forlenza (who discloses research/consulting support from Dexcom and others) criticized the report for relying on emotional anecdotes, lacking peer review and omitting G7 accuracy studies. — [Forlenza letter](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/15209156251390814); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41194687/)
- Dexcom management response (Oct 30, 2025 call): complaint rates "largely stable," accuracy complaints decreased; sensors "exceptional." — [Q3 2025 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/04/08/dexcom-dxcm-q3-2025-earnings-call-transcript/)

### Inferences
- The FDA letter independently corroborates the core of Hunterbrook's claim (an uncleared material change that reduced accuracy in Dexcom's own studies); the December 2023 date, complaint-rate statistics and causal links to deaths are Hunterbrook's and remain unverified.

### Gaps
- No formal written Dexcom rebuttal to Hunterbrook was found. No independent (non-Hunterbrook, non-Dexcom) MAUDE trend analysis for 2025-2026 was found.

## 7. Abbott patent litigation: history, 2024 verdicts, settlement terms

### Takeaway
Multi-jurisdiction patent war (US, UK, Germany, patent offices) ended with a December 20, 2024 global settlement and royalty-free cross-license plus a 10-year mutual covenant not to sue (to Dec 20, 2034) and no payments either way. Before settling, a March 22, 2024 Delaware jury returned a mixed verdict (Dexcom infringed one Abbott patent, not willful, no damages) and in November 2024 the judge found infringement on the hung-jury patent.

### Cited Findings
- March 2024 Delaware trial (Abbott v. DexCom, D. Del. 23-cv-239): on March 22, 2024 the jury found Dexcom infringed one patent, did not infringe a second, did not infringe a third (also found invalid), and hung on a fourth; claims were limited to the G6 inserter mechanism and wearable seal/mount; infringement not willful; no damages awarded. — [Dexcom 10-Q Q1 2024](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355724000079/dxcm-20240331.htm); [Law360](https://www.law360.com/articles/1816415/jury-says-dexcom-infringed-1-abbott-patent-in-mixed-verdict); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/dexcom-infringes-abbott-glucose-tracker-patent-in-mixed-verdict)

## 11. Manufacturing footprint and tariff exposure

### Takeaway
Production is in Mesa, AZ and Penang/Batu Kawan, Malaysia, with the $325M Athenry, Ireland plant officially opened April 20, 2026 (construction began Jan 2024) and ramping in 2H 2026 (hiring-before-output drag flagged for Q3 2026). Management has said direct tariff exposure is limited given US manufacturing and historical medtech exemptions; Malaysia received a retroactive tax holiday (certified July 2025).

### Cited Findings
- "We primarily manufacture our products at our manufacturing facilities in Mesa, Arizona and Penang, Malaysia. We are currently building out a new manufacturing facility in Athenry, Ireland," expected to "add substantial manufacturing capacity." — [Dexcom 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355726000027/dxcm-20251231.htm)
- Athenry: opened April 20, 2026; $325M investment; 1,000+ jobs; ~25-month build from Jan 2024; primarily G7; first plant outside the US. — [Drug Delivery Business](https://www.drugdeliverybusiness.com/dexcom-opens-athenry-cgm-manufacturing-facility/); [IDA Ireland](https://www.idaireland.com/latest-news/press-release/dexcom-selects-ireland-for-first-european-manufacturing-site)
- Q2 2026: "we will be doing quite a bit of hiring in Ireland here into the third quarter, and before turning on those lines, and those folks are not producing" (near-term margin headwind). — [Q2 2026 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/03/dexcom-dxcm-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)
- Tariffs (Q1 2025 call): Dexcom does not expect a large direct tariff impact given its diverse footprint, large US manufacturing presence (Mesa, San Diego) and the industry's historical exemptions, while watching indirect supply-cost effects. — [Q1 2025 call transcript (Equibles)](https://equibles.com/stocks/dxcm/calls/2025-q1)
- Malaysia: in July 2025 MIDA certified milestone achievement for an income-tax holiday, with a tax benefit recorded retroactive to Jan 1, 2024. — [Dexcom 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355726000027/dxcm-20251231.htm)
- Q3 2025: shift from expedited air to ocean freight began late Q3 2025 with further savings expected in 2026; scrap rates elevated but improving. — [Q3 2025 transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/04/08/dexcom-dxcm-q3-2025-earnings-call-transcript/)
