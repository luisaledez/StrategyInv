# CGM Market and Competitive Landscape for Dexcom (DXCM) — as of September 27, 2026

Scope note: Research covered Dexcom, Abbott, Medtronic/MiniMed, Roche, Senseonics, Chinese entrants, Apple/Samsung, reimbursement (CMS NCD and DMEPOS competitive bidding), GLP-1 interaction, international and pricing. Several primary pages were blocked (HME News and Modern Healthcare returned 403; Yahoo's Abbott Q2-2026 transcript returned 404), so some Abbott color comes from Abbott's newsroom, PR Newswire release and the Motley Fool Q1-2026 transcript instead.

Key headline numbers (all sourced below):
- Dexcom: FY2025 revenue $4.662B (+16%); Q1-26 $1.192B (+15% reported / +12% organic); Q2-26 $1.308B (+13% / +12% organic); FY26 guide $5.18–5.25B (+11–13%); ~3.5M active customers at end-2025.
- Abbott: Q2-26 Diabetes Care $2.188B (+10.5% reported / +9.0% comparable), CGM +11.0% reported / +9.5% comparable; Q1-26 CGM ~$2.0B, +7.6% comparable; Libre serves >7M users.
- Abbott's organic CGM growth decelerated from ~15–20% in 2025 to 7.6%–9.5% in H1-2026 while Dexcom held ~12% organic — i.e., Dexcom appears to be regaining share in 2026 after losing it in 2023–2025.
- Medicare non-insulin Type 2 NCD decision expected by CMS before end-2026, effective mid-2027 (per Dexcom CEO); ~25M US non-insulin T2 patients, ~10–12M of Medicare age.
- DMEPOS competitive bidding final rule (Nov 28, 2025) brings Class II CGMs into bidding with a $272.69/month bid ceiling, 75th-percentile pricing, program launch no later than Jan 1, 2028.

## Q1. Global and US CGM market size, growth, and penetration by population

### Takeaway
Third-party estimates put the global CGM market at roughly $14–16B in 2025–2026 growing ~10–15% per year toward $16–32B by 2030–2031 (wide dispersion between vendors); the two leaders' own revenues (Abbott CGM ~$8B run-rate + Dexcom ~$5.2B) already imply a >$13B revenue pool in 2026. Penetration remains highest in Type 1 (~60–65% US) and lowest in non-insulin Type 2 and prediabetes; Abbott's CEO frames the global addressable base at 70–80M people vs ~10–12M current users.

- Penetration figures for non-insulin T2 and prediabetes/wellness are not disclosed by either company; given ~25M US non-insulin T2 and Stelo's 140k users at end-2024, non-insulin penetration is almost certainly in the low single digits.

## Q2. Abbott FreeStyle Libre: revenue, growth, users, share vs Dexcom; pipeline; Medtronic partnership and pump-channel impact

### Takeaway
Abbott's CGM franchise (~$8B run-rate, >7M users) is larger than Dexcom's globally and dominant internationally, but its organic growth slowed sharply to 7.6% (Q1-26) and 9.5% (Q2-26) versus Dexcom's ~12% organic, so Abbott's 2023–2025 share gains have stalled or reversed in 2026. Abbott's pipeline (Libre Duo glucose-ketone, Libre 5) and its expanding pump integrations (Medtronic Instinct, Omnipod 5 Libre 3 Plus, Tandem, Beta Bionics, Sequel) erode Dexcom's historical exclusivity in the AID channel.

### Inferences
- Share trajectory: 2024–2025 Abbott CGM grew ~20% vs Dexcom 11% (2024) and 16% (2025), so Abbott took share. In H1-2026 the order flipped (Abbott 7.6%/9.5% comparable vs Dexcom 12%/12% organic), implying Dexcom is now gaining share, helped by G7 15 Day, record new-patient starts and non-insulin T2 commercial coverage. Abbott attributes part of its slowdown to a one-off international tender delay, so the reversal may be partly transitory.
- By revenue in the US, Dexcom (Q2-26 US $933M) is larger than Abbott's entire US Diabetes Care segment ($862M, which also includes BGM), so Dexcom likely holds >50% US CGM revenue share even if unit share is ~45%; internationally Abbott ($1,326M) is ~3.5x Dexcom ($375M).
- Pump-channel exclusivity is gone: every major AID system (Omnipod 5, t:slim X2, iLet, twiist, MiniMed 780G) now supports or will support a Libre sensor, and Libre Duo adds a ketone feature Dexcom cannot match until G8 (late 2027/early 2028). Dexcom's remaining pump-channel advantages are incumbency, G7 15 Day availability now, and the Medtronic channel being Abbott-exclusive is a net loss for Dexcom that was never Dexcom's to begin with.

### Gaps
- Abbott does not disclose Libre user counts, unit share, or pricing by quarter; FY2025 total Libre sales figure was not retrieved from a primary source (Q4-25 ~$2B, +15% is from a secondary source).
- No data on the sensor mix (Dexcom vs Libre) among Omnipod 5 or Tandem users.
- No confirmed Libre 5 timing beyond "in development."

## Q3. Other competitors: Medtronic/MiniMed Simplera, Roche, Senseonics, Chinese entrants, Apple/Samsung

### Takeaway
None of the secondary competitors is yet a material threat to Dexcom's US position: MiniMed (IPO Feb 2026, ~$2.7B revenue, net loss) sells Simplera mainly to its own pump base and now also uses Abbott's sensor; Roche's SmartGuide and Senseonics' Eversense 365 are niche (Senseonics guides $60–64M for 2026); Chinese makers (Sinocare, Sibionics, Yuwell, Medtrum) sell at 30–50% discounts in Europe and are the main structural pricing threat internationally; no non-invasive Apple/Samsung sensor exists or is expected near-term.

- MiniMed IPO priced Feb 24, 2026 at $25–28/share, 28M shares, up to $784M raised, Nasdaq: MMED; FY2025 revenue $2.7B, net loss $198M; Medtronic retains ~90%; pipeline includes MiniMed Flex (FDA submitted) and MiniMed Fit patch pump (filing by fall 2026); FDA cleared Simplera Sync with pumps and 780G with Abbott Instinct — [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/medtronics-minimed-prices-ipo-at-up-to-784m/812972/)

### Inferences
- MiniMed's decision to also carry Abbott's Instinct suggests Simplera alone was not competitive enough to protect its pump base; MiniMed as an independent company is a pump competitor to Dexcom's partners more than a CGM competitor to Dexcom.
- The Chinese entrants matter mainly for international tender pricing (Europe, Middle East, APAC) where Dexcom ONE+ already competes on price; Medtrum reimbursement in 20+ European countries shows payers are willing to list low-cost alternatives, which constrains Dexcom's international ASPs.
- Non-invasive smartwatch sensing is not a 2026–2028 threat; the near-term "wellness" competition is OTC arm-worn sensors (Stelo vs Lingo/Libre Rio).

- G7 15 Day is margin-accretive (fewer sensors per patient-month at similar monthly pricing) and is the main driver of the ~600 bps YoY gross-margin recovery; it also neutralizes Libre's prior wear-time advantage (Libre 3 Plus 15 days).
- Dexcom's dual-analyte answer (G8) arrives 12–18 months after Libre Duo's US launch, so Abbott will hold a ketone-sensing exclusivity window through at least 2027, most relevant to Type 1 and SGLT2 users.

## Q5. Reimbursement: Medicare/commercial non-insulin T2 coverage, pharmacy vs DME channel, CMS competitive bidding

### Takeaway
Commercial coverage for non-insulin Type 2 is now in place at the four largest PBMs (>7M covered lives), and a Medicare NCD decision is expected before end-2026 with effect mid-2027 (management guidance, not yet a published CMS decision as of this research); the offsetting risk is the November 2025 DMEPOS final rule that pulls Class II CGMs into competitive bidding (bid ceiling $272.69/month, 75th-percentile pricing, launch no later than Jan 1, 2028).

### Cited Findings
- April 2023 CMS LCD expansion covered CGM for any insulin user and for non-insulin patients with problematic hypoglycemia — [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/Medicare-CGM-coverage-Dexcom-abbott-ABT-DXCM/644019/); [diaTribe](https://diatribe.org/diabetes-management/medicare-expands-cgm-coverage-people-type-2-diabetes)
- Current Medicare criteria for non-insulin users: documented history of at least one Level 2 hypoglycemic event (<54 mg/dL) persisting despite more than one treatment-plan change — [CoveredUSA](https://coveredusa.org/en/qa/does-medicare-cover-cgm)
- Dexcom asked CMS to expand coverage to non-insulin T2; CMS was expected to propose in H1-2026; Dexcom's 300-person RCT (CONNECT) read out mid-2026; commercial coverage began unlocking in late 2025; ADA guidelines updated to recommend considering CGM for T2 on non-insulin glucose-lowering meds; RBC's Shagun Singh noted 2026 guidance assumes no benefit from T2 coverage expansion — [MedTech Dive, Feb 13 2026](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-seeks-expanded-medicare-coverage-of-cgms-for-type-2-diabetes/812223/)
- Q2-26 call (late July 2026): CMS decision expected "before the end of this year," effective mid-2027; CONNECT trial: 1.6% A1C improvement in CGM arm over six months, 0.9% vs control, 97% median wear rate over 26 weeks; ~5 more hours/day in range — [AOL Q2-26 transcript](https://www.aol.com/articles/dexcom-dxcm-q2-2026-earnings-001522000.html); [MedTech Dive, Aug 27 2026](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-abbott-await-medicare-coverage-expansion-for-cgms/828775/)
- Abbott's Ford: expansion "could be a multibillion-dollar opportunity, and it could happen in the fall"; ~10M more Medicare recipients eligible — [MedTech Dive, Aug 27 2026](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/dexcom-abbott-await-medicare-coverage-expansion-for-cgms/828775/)
- Commercial: coverage for all people with diabetes across the four largest PBMs (Prime Therapeutics national formulary win effective summer 2026 added to the prior three), >7M non-insulin T2 lives — [AOL Q1-26 transcript](https://www.aol.com/finance/dexcom-dxcm-q1-2026-earnings-223343295.html); [AOL Q2-26 transcript](https://www.aol.com/articles/dexcom-dxcm-q2-2026-earnings-001522000.html). One major insurer dropped prior authorization for CGM on commercial products effective 3/1/2026 — [BCBS Rhode Island policy](https://www.bcbsri.com/providers/sites/providers/files/policies/2026/01/2026%20DRAFT%20Glucose%20Monitoring%20-%20Continuous.Eff_.3.1.2026.pdf)
- No CMS proposed decision memo for non-insulin T2 CGM coverage surfaced in searches of the Medicare Coverage Database as of late September 2026 — [CMS MCD search](https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/ncacal-decision-memo.aspx?proposed=Y&ncaid=231&keyword=continuous+glucose+monitor&keywordType=starts&areaId=all&docType=NCA,CAL,NCD,MEDCAC,TA,MCD,6,3,5,1,F,P&contractOption=all&sortBy=relevance&bc=1)
- DMEPOS competitive bidding final rule: finalized Nov 28, 2025 (Federal Register Dec 2, 2025); program launch "no later than January 1, 2028" with a 6-month supplier-transition window; CGMs and insulin pumps move to monthly rental under the frequent-and-substantial-servicing category; bid ceilings CGM $272.69/month, insulin pumps $226.22/month; single payment amount set at the 75th percentile of winning bids (down from the maximum winning bid); remote item delivery (RID) national/regional category covers CGMs, pumps, urological, ostomy, braces; at least 30% of contracts to small suppliers; diabetes industry groups "strongly opposed" inclusion but CMS proceeded largely unchanged — [Applied Policy](https://www.appliedpolicy.com/cms-finalizes-rule-on-durable-medical-equipment-prosthetics-orthotics-and-supplies-dmepos-competitive-bidding-program-without-major-changes/)
- CMS's bid-ceiling math: average Class II CGM purchase fee divided by 60 plus supply fee = 2025 monthly ceiling of $272.69; based on 2024 claims CMS expects ~10 national contract suppliers in the CGM/insulin pump category — [XiFin](https://www.xifin.com/billing-beat/cms-announces-major-updates-to-dmepos-competitive-bidding-program/)
- The rule aligns with an OIG report finding Medicare significantly overpaid for CGMs — [McKnights Home Care](https://www.mcknightshomecare.com/news/cms-reboots-competitive-bidding-program-in-2026-home-health-final-rule/)
- Dexcom channel mix: Q2-26 distributor revenue $1,102.4M (US $893.5M, intl $208.9M) vs direct $206.0M (US $39.9M, intl $166.1M) — distributors ~84% of revenue — [Dexcom Q2-26 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001093557/000109355726000143/dxcm-20260630.htm)
- Dexcom describes itself as "the most covered and reimbursed CGM brand," with G7 15 Day meeting Medicare therapeutic-CGM category requirements — [Dexcom press release](https://investors.dexcom.com/news/news-details/2025/Dexcom-G7-15-Day-Continuous-Glucose-Monitoring-System-to-Launch-on-Dec--1-in-the-United-States/default.aspx)

### Inferences
- The competitive bidding rule applies to Medicare Part B DME; CGM dispensed through Part D pharmacy benefit is outside its scope, so the DME-to-pharmacy shift Dexcom has pursued since 2023 partly insulates it. The rule also makes CGM a monthly rental with no 5-year replacement lock-in, which lowers switching costs between brands for Medicare patients.
- With a $272.69/month ceiling and 75th-percentile pricing, Medicare DME reimbursement for CGM is likely to fall from 2028; the magnitude depends on bids by ~10 national suppliers (distributors such as Byram, Edgepark, etc.), who would in turn pressure manufacturer pricing. Neither Dexcom nor Abbott quantified the exposure on their calls.
- Management's timing (decision by YE2026, effective mid-2027) implies the Medicare non-insulin T2 tailwind is a 2027–2028 story; 2026 guidance excludes it (per RBC).

### Gaps
- No published CMS proposed decision memo or NCD tracking sheet located; the timeline rests on Dexcom/Abbott management statements.
- No Dexcom disclosure of the pharmacy vs DME revenue split or the ASP differential between channels.
- No estimate of Dexcom's Medicare-DME revenue share (needed to size competitive-bidding risk).

## Q6. GLP-1 drugs and CGM adoption

### Takeaway
Available evidence points to GLP-1s being a net tailwind: Dexcom claims CGM use nearly quadrupled among non-insulin users after GLP-1 initiation, claims-based studies show larger A1C reductions with CGM plus GLP-1 than GLP-1 alone, and CONNECT's largest relative A1C gain was in the GLP-1-only cohort.

### Cited Findings
- Dexcom data: CGM use nearly quadrupled among non-insulin users after starting a GLP-1 — [Dexcom](https://www.dexcom.com/en-us/all-access/clinical-corner/benefits-of-glp-1-drugs)
- Claims study in T2D with A1C >8%: Dexcom CGM plus GLP-1 produced larger A1C reductions than GLP-1 alone — [Dexcom](https://www.dexcom.com/en-us/glp-1-and-dexcom-g7-managing-diabetes-better-together)
- Peer-reviewed analysis of combined CGM and anti-diabetes medication use in non-insulin T2D — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12375899/)
- CONNECT (ADA 2026): "the largest relative improvement in A1C was within the cohort using only GLP-1 therapies," reinforcing "the complementary relationship between CGM and incretin therapy" — [AOL Q2-26 transcript](https://www.aol.com/articles/dexcom-dxcm-q2-2026-earnings-001522000.html)
- Dexcom CEO Jake Leach publicly argues diabetics on GLP-1s should also wear CGMs (Feb 2026) — [MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/2026/02/dexcoms-ceo-believes-diabetics-on-glp-1s-should-also-wear-cgms/)
- Abbott's Libre bull case cites "obesity drug tailwinds (GLP-1 patients still need glucose monitoring)" — [24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/04/17/abbot-price-prediction-where-will-the-stock-be-in-2027/)

### Inferences
- The quadrupling statistic is company-generated and likely reflects that GLP-1 initiation coincides with a physician touchpoint and increased engagement; it does not isolate causality. Still, no source found evidence of GLP-1s reducing CGM adoption.
- The long-run bear case (GLP-1s reduce progression to insulin, shrinking the intensive-insulin CGM pool) was not addressed quantitatively by any source found.

### Gaps

Dexcom does not disclose ASP, but revenue growth (+16% in 2025) trailing active-customer growth (+20%+) implies per-customer revenue fell roughly 3–5% in 2025, driven by mix toward Type 2/basal, pharmacy-channel rebates, international ONE+/Flex tiers, and Stelo; competitive bidding (2028) and Medicare non-insulin coverage (2027, lower-intensity users) are the next sources of per-patient dilution, partly offset by G7 15 Day's cost-per-day advantage.
